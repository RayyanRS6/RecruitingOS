# Administrator guide

**ITS AI Recruiting Department, Founding Partner Program. Release 1.**

Draft of 30 September 2026, brought up to date with the product on 15 October 2026, for review by ITS before it is given to a customer. This guide is for the account owner: the person who creates the workspace, invites the team, and is responsible for the settings. Admins can do everything in this guide except make someone an owner, and can use it too. If you also run hires yourself, read the hiring manager guide as well.

Screen names and button labels in **bold** are the ones you will see in the product.

## 1. Signing in

1. Open the sign-in page your ITS contact gave you.
2. Press **Continue with Google** or **Continue with Microsoft**. Use the work account your company controls.
3. Your Google or Microsoft account handles the sign-in, including any two-step verification you have turned on there. ITS never sees or keeps a password.
4. The first time, you land on **Tell us about your company** (section 2). After that you land on **Home**.

Your session lasts up to thirty days of inactivity. Press **Sign out** at the bottom of the left rail (at the end of the top bar on a phone or tablet) to end it sooner. **Your sessions** in **Settings** (on the **Security** tab) lists every place you are signed in (section 5).

If you see "Email me a sign-in link" on the sign-in page, ignore it. It is only offered in some setups, and your workspace uses Google or Microsoft.

## 2. Creating the company

Only one person does this: the owner. Fill in **Tell us about your company**:

- **Company name**
- **Your name**
- **What kind of business**: pick the closest. The hint under it says whether we have a hiring guide for that kind of business (today: consumer brands, retail, agencies, software, professional services and dental practices) or whether your roles are drafted from what you tell the assistant and public pay data.
- **How many employees**
- **City** and **State** (two letters)
- **Time zone**: filled in from your computer. Interview times and the hours candidates may be texted use it, so check it.
- **Website (optional)**

Press **Create my hiring workspace**. You are now the owner. Everything you invite others into lives inside this workspace. If you signed in with the wrong account, press **Sign out** at the top of the form.

To change these details later, and to add more, use the **Company** card in **Settings** (section 5).

## 3. The screens at a glance

At the top of the left rail is your company, with your role in it and a cog that opens **Settings**. If you belong to more than one company, a **Switch company** button sits next to it: it lists your companies, and picking one moves you there and opens its Home (nothing from the company you left stays on screen). Below it, for owners, admins and hiring managers, is **Start a hire**: it opens the job builder, a page of its own where a new job is drafted with the assistant (or the draft you already started). Nothing is saved until you send your first message. Pressing **N** on any page does the same, unless you are typing in a box or have changed something on that page (so nothing you were working on is lost); you can turn that key off in **Settings**, on the **Notifications** tab, under **Keyboard shortcut**.

The rail then has five entries:

- **Home**: one line on how many things need you, then **Needs you** (a short row per item, with its buttons; **Why** opens the rest), **Your jobs**, the **Who are you hiring?** box that hands your first words to the job builder, your recruiter requests, and **Recent activity** (folded).
- **Jobs**: every job, its status and its counts. Up to eight jobs you are working on are listed under it: a green dot and the number of applicants for a live job, a grey dot for a paused job, a draft or one waiting for approval. **All jobs** shows the rest. A live job opens on its **Candidates**; a draft opens on the job itself. For an interviewer, this list and the **Jobs** page show only the jobs they interview for, without applicant counts.
- **Candidates**: the candidates for a job, on a stage strip (All, New, Shortlist, Interviewing, Hired and Not moving forward, plus any other stage with someone in it; the rest, such as Longlist, Cancelled, On hold and Offer, sit under **More stages**) with each one's match score, and a pool board that sorts them for you to review. **Cancelled** holds the candidates who cancelled a booked interview or did not come to one, with what each chose. The hiring manager guide covers it.
- **Interviews**: every interview coming up or done, and each interviewer's load.
- **Settings**: five tabs. **Company** (your company, its plan and usage, and how candidates see you); **Team**; **Notifications** (email alerts, your availability for interviews, the keyboard shortcut); **Hiring rules** (what the assistant may do on its own, when a candidate cancels, job boards); and **Security** (your sessions, ITS recruiter access, connected services, the audit history). Older links to a card in Settings open the right tab.

Each job has one tab strip, the same on all its pages: **Candidates** (with how many applied), **Job** (the details), **Scoring** (the requirements and how candidates are evaluated), **Interview plan**, **Share** (the public link first, then the boards, tracked links and closing date), **Report** and **Activity** (what happened, and every version). The job's **More** menu holds Edit, Candidate view, Pause or Resume, Stop accepting applications, and Close the job.

Your name, your email address and **Sign out** are at the bottom. On a job's own pages the rail folds to a narrow column of icons to leave room for the work. On a phone or a tablet the rail becomes a bar across the top: **Start a hire** and **Sign out** stay in it, and **Menu** opens the rest.

**Bring in a recruiter** appears on Home, on every job and on every candidate.

## 4. Inviting your team and choosing roles

In **Settings**, on the **Team** tab, enter a work email under **Invite by email**, add their name if you like, choose a **Role**, and press **Invite**. The person gets one email. The link works for seven days. They open it and sign in with Google or Microsoft using the same work address; opening the link alone signs nobody in, and it does not work for a different address.

People who have not signed in yet are listed under **Invited, not yet in**, with who invited them and until when the link works. Only owners, admins and HR see this list. **Send again** sends a new link for another seven days (the old one stops working). **Withdraw** stops the link at once. An invitation to become an owner is sent again or withdrawn only by an owner, and one to become an admin only by an owner or an admin; HR sees no buttons on those. Inviting the same address again replaces the open invitation, so the same rule applies to that.

| Role | Best for | Can | Cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Owner** | You, and anyone else who should hold the account | Everything, including making other owners | Remove the last owner (there is always at least one) |
| **Admin** | A second person who runs the account with you | Everything the owner can: company settings, team, roles, postings, candidates, pay, rules, recruiter access, outcomes, the audit history | Make someone an owner or change an owner's role |
| **Hiring manager** | The person who runs a hire | Start a role in the conversation, edit and approve the packet, see candidates and pay, invite to a screen, hold, approve interviews, record the decision after an interview, decline, write to candidates, record outcomes and close a role, bring in a recruiter, see the timeline and the report | Change the team, change what the assistant may do, end a recruiter's access, pause a role, change postings, stop accepting applications |
| **Interviewer** | Anyone who meets candidates but does not decide | See the roles they interview for and their own interviews, with a short brief on each candidate they interview; say whether an interview took place; set their own availability | See pay, the candidate list or other candidates; act on candidates; see the report |
| **HR** | Someone who keeps records and manages the team | See roles, candidates and pay; invite to a screen, hold; write to candidates; record an offer made, a hire and other outcomes, and close a role; keep team notes; say whether an interview they sat on took place; bring in a recruiter; invite, change and remove team members | Approve a role; edit a packet; decline a candidate or send a decision or closing message; approve an interview or decide after one |

**Changing a role.** Pick the new role from the list next to the person and press **Change role**. They are signed out of this company and get the new role when they sign in again.

**Signing someone out.** Owners and admins see **Sign out everywhere** next to each teammate (an admin does not see it next to an owner, and nobody sees it next to themselves). Press it, then **Sign (name) out**. Every session they have open in this company ends, on every device, and they must sign in again to work here. They stay on the team with the same role. Use it after a lost or shared device. If they use the same account for another company, their sessions there stay open, and from those they could come back here: to keep them out, remove them from the team. HR cannot sign a teammate out. To end your own other sessions, use **Sign out everywhere else** under **Your sessions** (section 5).

**Removing someone.** Press **Remove** next to the person, then **Remove (name)**. They lose access at once and are signed out of this company. Their calendar connection is disconnected and deleted. They are taken off every interview still ahead. An interview where they were the only interviewer is cancelled, and a candidate who had already booked a time is told. To go on with that candidate, approve a new interview with another interviewer from the candidate's page. You get one message listing which interviews were cancelled and which go ahead. What they did stays in the record.

**Leaving a company yourself.** Anyone on the team sees **Leave this company** on their own row. Press it, read what happens, then press **Leave this company** again (or **Stay**). It works like a removal: you lose access at once, your calendar connection here is disconnected, interviews you were going to run alone are cancelled and the team is told, and the owners are told you left. If you belong to another company you move to it; otherwise you are signed out. An owner can leave only while another owner remains. To close the whole company, ask ITS.

Notes:

- Owners, admins and HR can invite, change and remove people. Only an owner can make someone an owner or change an owner's role, and only an owner or an admin can make someone an admin or change an admin's role. Only owners and admins can sign a teammate out. Nobody can change their own role or remove themselves, but anyone can leave (an owner only while another owner remains).
- ITS recruiters do not need an invitation, and an ITS address cannot be invited. They get access only through **Bring in a recruiter** (section 8).
- Interviewers never see pay. Hiring managers can approve roles and decide on candidates.
- Every invitation, change of role, sign-out and removal is recorded in the audit history.

## 5. The Settings screen

Settings has five tabs, and each card sits on one of them: **Company** (Company, with your plan and its usage; How candidates see you), **Team**, **Notifications** (Email alerts, Your availability, Keyboard shortcut), **Hiring rules** (What the AI may do, When a candidate cancels, Job boards) and **Security** (Your sessions, ITS recruiter access, Connected services, Audit history). Each card says what it is for in one line; the longer explanation is under **How this works**. A tab shows only the cards you can use, and an interviewer's Settings opens on **Notifications**. A link to a card from an email, a card on Home or a message opens its tab and scrolls to it.

**Keyboard shortcut.** For the people who may start a hire: **Press N to start a hire** is on unless you untick it. The choice is kept in the browser you are using.

**Company.** Your name, the ITS check, careers page address, website, kind of business, size, time zone, locations, contact hours, candidate notices, plan and usage. **ITS check** shows **Confirmed**, or **ITS is confirming your company** while the one-time check is under way; until then the careers page address carries "Offline until ITS confirms your company." **Plan** shows your program and, where it has an end date, that date; a role already in progress is never cut off on that date. **Usage** shows what your program includes and what you have used, recruiter requests among them: only the requests your own team makes count, and going over never holds one up. The monthly allowances (screening chats and recruiter requests) start again from zero every 30 days, and the last line says when: "Monthly allowances reset on October 30." Applicants who were waiting only because the screening chats were used up get their link on their own after the reset.

Owners and admins press **Edit** on the card to change the company name, website, logo address, time zone, size, locations, the careers-page introduction, the benefits summary, the contact hours and the wording of the candidate notices, then **Save changes**. The website and logo appear on your job pages and in search results. Contact hours can only narrow the legal window for texting candidates, never widen it. A reworded notice gets a new version, shows on the job page and the application form from then on, and is recorded; clear the box to go back to the standard wording. The plan, the program dates and the allowances are set by ITS and cannot be changed here. Every change is recorded in the audit history.

**Team.** The people in your workspace, each with their role and an availability chip: "google calendar", "hours declared" or "no availability". Someone with "no availability" cannot be offered as an interviewer until they set it. The invitation form, **Invited, not yet in**, the role and removal controls and **Sign out everywhere** are here (section 4).

**ITS recruiter access.** Who from ITS has access right now, to what, and until when. This is empty most of the time. See section 8 for how to end access.

**Your sessions.** Every place you are signed in: the device, how you signed in and when it was last used. **Sign out** ends one; **Sign out everywhere else** ends all but the one you are using. If you see one you do not recognize, sign it out and tell support.

**Confirm it's you.** Changing what the assistant may do, the wording of candidate notices, or the automatic new times under **When a candidate cancels** needs a recent sign-in. If you signed in more than 30 minutes ago, the product shows **Confirm it's you** and a **Confirm with my sign-in** button. It sends you through Google or Microsoft again and brings you straight back; for the next ten minutes you can make the change. Confirming with a different account changes nothing.

**My availability for interviews.** Each person sets their own. Two ways:

- **Declared hours**: time zone, the days and hours you can interview, **Buffer between interviews (minutes)** and **Most interviews per day**. Press **Save availability**.
- **Calendar**: if ITS has enabled Google Calendar for your workspace you see **Connect Google Calendar**. The product asks only for your events and free or busy times. **Disconnect** deletes the credential and you can see when that is done. If it is not enabled you see "Google Calendar is not configured for this deployment yet"; use declared hours. Microsoft calendars cannot be connected in release 1.

If a person has both, the declared hours limit the calendar.

**Email alerts.** Each person chooses for themselves how they hear about updates by email, group by group: **Waiting for your approval**, **Candidates**, **Interviews** (your calendar connection included), **Roles that need attention**, **Your ITS recruiter**, **Messages to candidates** and **Your account**. For each: **As it happens** (one short email per update), **Daily summary** (8:00 each morning, your company's time), **Weekly summary** (Monday at 8:00) or **Off**. The card says which updates are always emailed at once and why: an ITS recruiter is given access to your hiring, ITS confirms your company or takes the confirmation back, a candidate asks for a person, or someone waits for your eligibility decision. Every update still shows in the product whatever you choose. Alert emails carry the role's name and a link, never a candidate's name or details. Unsubscribing from an alert email turns your alerts off here.

**Connected services.** A table of the services the product uses for your company, each with a status: **Connected and working**, **Submitted, awaiting verification**, **Manual action required**, **Not connected** or **Disabled**. The last column explains what each one takes. Two rows to understand:

- The job boards (LinkedIn, Indeed, ZipRecruiter) show **Manual action required** or **Not connected**. This is expected. ITS posts to them by hand under your accounts, or your team does if you choose **Our team posts them** under **Job boards**. Tick **We have our own paid ZipRecruiter plan** there to have ZipRecruiter offered on your roles. Whether a particular role is live on a board is shown on that role's **Share** tab (section 7), not here.
- Text messaging shows as off. Screening runs by web chat from an email link.

The line under the table names the AI provider. On your live workspace it reads Anthropic.

**When a candidate cancels.** When candidates cancel a booked interview from their scheduling page, they choose "Send me new times later" or "I'm no longer interested", and go under **Cancelled** on the job's Candidates page with that choice. Candidates who did not come to an interview go there too, as a no-show. This card decides what happens next for the people who asked for new times:

- **Send new times automatically to people who ask for them** (on unless you untick it). Each person who asked gets one set of new times by email, for the same interview with the same interviewers, after the number of working days in **Working days to wait** (1 to 10, 3 unless you change it; weekends and holidays are not counted, and the email goes out in the morning, your company's time). Press **Save** after changing the number. If they do not pick a time, they stay under Cancelled for you to decide; there is never a second automatic set.
- Nothing ever goes automatically to people who said they are no longer interested, people who did not come, someone who had already moved the interview twice, someone you had put on hold, anyone while an ITS recruiter is running the role, or anyone before ITS has confirmed your company (the card says so until then). A reply of HUMAN or "no thanks" stops a set that was due.
- **Switching it off** while some people were already told a day for their new times asks you first what should happen to them: **Still send them on the day they were told**, or **Stop them too; the team will be in touch**. Nothing is chosen for you. Changing the number of days never moves anyone already told a day; it applies to cancellations from then on.
- Only owners and admins change it, after a recent sign-in (**Confirm it's you**, above); everyone else sees one line saying what is set. Every change is recorded in the audit history.

**What the AI may do on its own.** A list of actions grouped by stage of hiring. Where a setting changes what happens, you choose one of four levels:

- **Do it**: the assistant does it without asking.
- **Do it and tell me**: the assistant does it and you see it under **Recent activity** on Home.
- **Ask me first**: the assistant proposes it and waits for a person to approve.
- **Only a person**: the assistant never does it.

Some rows are marked **always a person**. They are locked at "Only a person" and nobody can change them: declining a candidate on judgment, declining an applicant held on an eligibility answer, deciding who to hire, negotiating an offer, acting on a background check, changing these rules, changing eligibility rules, changing candidate notices, deleting candidate data, and exporting data. "Approve a drafted role, pay included" is always **Ask me first**: a person approves every role before it opens.

Some rows show **Nothing to choose** with a line saying what the product does, because no setting changes it: reading and assessing applicants (information only; it never decides anything), running the screening conversation once someone is invited, sending confirmations and reminders, advancing someone to an interview (a person always approves it on the candidate's page), and booking and moving interviews on calendars once a person approved them (including the one automatic set of new times after a candidate cancels, which you switch on or off under **When a candidate cancels**, above).

The defaults are the recommended settings. The ones you are most likely to touch:

- **Invite an applicant to the screening chat** (default: Do it and tell me). Set it to Ask me first if you want to approve every screening invitation.
- **Recommend who to interview** (default: Do it and tell me).
- **Send a message a team member wrote to a candidate** (default: Do it and tell me: the message goes at once and owners are told). Set it to Ask me first or Only a person and an owner or admin reads each message before it goes out. Messages from owners and admins always go at once.
- **Send the decision message** (default: Do it: the message goes when someone on your team presses Send, after 30 seconds in which they can undo it; declining never sends anything by itself). Under Do it, someone nobody has told five working days after the decision gets the standard message by itself and you are told; under Ask me first or Only a person, or while an ITS recruiter runs the role, an ITS recruiter is brought in instead and nothing is sent. The same setting covers the one closing message when a role closes (Ask me first: one approval for the whole group).
- **Send a "still reviewing" note** (default: Only a person, so it is off and no note goes by itself: with many applicants you pick the best few and keep in touch yourself). Choose Do it to switch it on: someone still waiting for a decision 30 days after applying then gets one short note saying a person is still reviewing it. A choice your company already saved stays as it is.

Only owners and admins can change these settings, and only after a recent sign-in (**Confirm it's you**, above). Choosing a level that lets more happen without a person asks you to confirm first; choosing a stricter one saves at once. Every change is recorded. Actions that are not part of release 1 (offers, background checks, references, payroll, outreach) are listed under **Coming later**, with nothing to choose.

**Audit history.** Who did what in your workspace, in plain words, newest first: recruiter access, team changes, rule changes, company changes, candidate records opened, outcomes and downloads. The last 50 are shown. Tick **Include every page an ITS recruiter opened** to see each page as well. **Download as a spreadsheet** gives you everything; the download is itself recorded. Owners, admins, hiring managers and HR can see it.

## 6. The daily brief

**Home** opens with a greeting and a one-line summary: how many things need you, who is worth a look, what is at risk, and what was handled for you. Below it are cards, most important first. Each card has a label:

- **Needs you**: candidates waiting for a decision, or applicants held on an eligibility answer.
- **Decision**: a role packet to approve, or a screen or interview the assistant proposed under "Ask me first".
- **New applicants**: how many people applied since yesterday and how many are ready for review.
- **One step**: a board where the job still has to be posted by hand, or a board where the posting is still up after you closed it ("Still posted on ...").
- **Decision** also covers a message a teammate wrote to a candidate that waits for you to read it, when your rules say so.
- **Screening**: who is invited, answering now, or completed since yesterday.
- **Stuck**: a role where nothing has moved for a while, with a suggested action and **Have a recruiter review**.
- **Recruiter**: a request you sent, shown as "A recruiter will pick this up" or "A recruiter is on it", with **See the request**.
- **Getting started**: for a new company, the setup steps each person can do (company details, team, interview hours, the first role, job-board access, the AI rules), each with a line on why it matters, ticked as they are done; **Hide these steps** hides them for you only. While ITS has not yet confirmed your company the card says so, with how many emails to candidates are waiting, and nobody can hide that part.
- **Ready to sort**: an open role with two or more people the pool board can sort, with a link to **Review and apply**.
- **Draft**: a draft of your own still in progress.
- **Closing date**: a role that stops taking applications tonight or tomorrow night, or has just stopped, with **Extend by two weeks**, **Choose another date** and, for people who may close a role, **Close the role**.
- **Live today**: a job that went public today, with **Share the job**.
- **New hire**: "Keep your backups warm" until a new hire's start date, naming the people still in the running.
- **Your interviews**: for an interviewer, an interview to say took place or not.
- **Nothing waiting**: nothing needs you. A new company with no role yet sees **Start a hire** here.

**Needs you** also covers, for people who may decline candidates, those whose decision has not been sent to them yet, with **Review and send** (anyone whose message already waits for approval is on the approver's card instead), as well as a hold that has ended and an offer with no answer recorded after five days. **Stuck** also names whoever has waited longest on a role, with **Decide**, **Hold until a date** and **Send a note**; nothing is sent from the card.

A role packet waiting for approval appears as a **Decision** only for the people who can approve it: not for the person who started the role (unless they are the only owner), and only for the named decision maker or an owner when the packet names one.

Home refreshes itself while it is open. Under the brief, **Your recruiter requests** lists your team's requests to ITS (section 8). **Recent activity** lists what happened since you last looked: an invitation sent, a resume that could not be read, an interview booked. It shows the eight newest. Press **Mark all read** when you have seen them; when there are more than eight it reads **Mark these read** and marks only the ones on screen. **See earlier updates** opens the **Updates** page: every update you ever had here, newest first, all or only the unread ones, each of which you can mark read or unread again. Its **Mark all read** marks only the unread updates on the page; while there are more further back it says how many it marks. **Email alerts** there leads to your settings in Settings.

Under the brief, people who can start a hire see the **Who are you hiring?** box: what is typed there opens the job builder (**Start a hire** in the menu goes there too), where a new role is drafted with the assistant. The hiring manager guide covers it. The brief shows each person their own **Draft in progress**.

**Who sees a draft.** A draft can be seen and worked on by the person who started it and by every owner and admin; nobody else on the team sees it. Owners and admins find the drafts others started under **Drafts on your team** on the **Jobs** page (under **All** or **Drafts**). Opening one lets you help with it; it stays the other person's. If they are away or have left, press **Take it over** on the draft: it becomes yours, the person who started it is told in their updates, and, unless they are an owner or an admin, they can no longer work on it. When someone brings in a recruiter from a draft, that recruiter sees the draft and its conversation too.

## 7. Jobs and posting

**Jobs** lists every job with a status: **Draft**, **Waiting for approval**, **Live**, **Paused**, **Filled** or **Closed**. One bar above the list narrows and orders it:

- **All**, **Live**, **Drafts**, **Paused** and **Closed**, each with how many jobs it holds. **Drafts** includes the jobs waiting for approval and your own drafts in progress; **Closed** includes the filled ones.
- **Search by title**.
- A sort: **Recent activity** (the default: the job something last happened on comes first), **Newest first**, **Oldest first** or **Title A to Z**.

What you choose is kept in the page's address, so a link you send opens the same view. Each row shows one line (how many applied and how many days it has been open, or "Not posted yet") and small badges: **N to review**, **N new**, **Draft** with its age, **Not live** (waiting for approval) and **No applicants yet**. An interviewer sees only the jobs they interview for, without these numbers. A live job's row opens its **Candidates**, with a small **Job details** link to the job page; a draft's row opens the job page, with a small **Candidates** link. Owners, admins and hiring managers also see **Post on LinkedIn** on each live job, once ITS has confirmed your company: it opens the job's **Share** tab at its LinkedIn card. Each filter that has nothing in it says so, with one button (for example **Show all jobs** or **Clear search**). **Start a hire** (for the people who may start one) opens the first page of a new job: **Answer a few quick questions** (ten short steps, one question each) or describe the job to the assistant; the hiring manager guide covers both. Below the jobs, **Still being drafted** lists your own drafts and, for owners and admins, **Drafts on your team** (folded) lists the ones others started. **Hire for this job again** on a filled or closed job starts a new draft from it; the pay is asked again.

**The job page** has the title, its status and one line of facts (place, type, pay) at the top, with **Bring in a recruiter** and a **More** menu (Edit, Candidate view, Pause or Resume, Stop accepting applications, Close the job, and Hire for this job again or Send the closing message once it is closed). Then one tab strip: **Candidates**, **Job** (the details), **Scoring** (the requirements and how we'll evaluate), **Interview plan**, **Share**, **Report**, **Activity**. **Edit** opens the editor; every save is a new version, and **Activity** lists them all. Nothing is overwritten. **Interview plan** shows who interviews in each round and whether times can be offered to them yet; **Activity** also holds everything that happened on the job, newest first, in plain words. Older links to the Overview, Requirements, Posting or History tab open the tab that now holds it.

**Approval.** A role waiting for approval shows a card **Approve this role packet** with any assumptions the assistant made. **Preview the job page** opens the version waiting exactly as candidates will see it, in a new tab (never public). The card also lists what the job page shows of the must-haves and nice-to-haves (marked **Candidates see this**), which job boards it goes to (owners and admins change the ticks there), what the law where the job is still asks of the ad (the pay range, a short description of the benefits, a closing date for Colorado or remote jobs; approving waits for these), and what starts on its own once it is live. **Approve and publish** opens the role: the careers page and Google come first, and each board follows once it is posted and its address checked. While ITS has not yet confirmed your company, the card says so: approving opens the role, but nothing of it is public until ITS confirms; the top of the job page and the Share tab explain why the job page shows "not found" meanwhile, and it goes public on its own once ITS confirms. **Edit first** lets you change the packet before you approve. Owners, admins and hiring managers can approve, with two rules: the person who ran the intake (or wrote the version being approved) cannot approve it themselves unless they are the company's only owner, and when the packet names a decision maker who is on your team, the approval is reserved for them (an owner can also decide). The card says who can decide and why you cannot, if you cannot. **Send back with a note** returns the role to a draft with the note on its page; **Request approval again** sends it back after the edit. An approval nobody decides within 14 days goes back to a draft the same way. An edit to an open role by someone who can edit but not approve waits for approval on the same card while the role keeps running on its current version; Home shows it as "Approve packet v3 of ...". If your company narrowed who approves jobs, only those people's edits take effect at once, and the approval notice names only them. When someone else's wording-only edit takes effect at once, the job's named decision maker is told. If the editor kept wording the fairness check flagged on a live or paused job, a second approver must say yes before it goes public, unless the editor is the company's only owner; the person who kept it cannot approve it. An edit saved while the job is paused follows the same rules and shows on the job page when you resume it. A closed job takes no new versions; the editor says so.

**The Share tab** (once called Posting). First the job's public link, with **Copy** and **Open**. Right under it, **Post on LinkedIn** writes a short announcement for your own LinkedIn feed from the job's facts (title, place, work model, pay when the job page shows it, up to three must-haves, and the job's LinkedIn link, so applicants from it are counted as LinkedIn). Pick a tone (**Proud**, **Straight** or **Friendly**), change the words in the box if you like (a preview and a character count sit beside it), then press **Copy and open LinkedIn**: the words are copied and LinkedIn opens in a new tab for you to paste them. **Copy text** only copies. Nothing is posted for you, and the product never connects to your LinkedIn account. The card works for a live job once ITS has confirmed your company; until then it says so. Then one card per place the job appears. Each card has a status and one sentence that says what happens next.

- **Careers page**: served by the product. The card shows the job page's address with **Copy**. The product opens the page and checks it before calling it **Live and verified**.
- **Google for Jobs**: the job page carries the data Google reads. Google lists jobs on its own schedule, so this shows **Submitted** until someone sees the job in Google and presses **Mark verified** with a short note.
- **Applications by email**: an address where forwarded applications arrive once ITS has connected incoming mail for your workspace. It shows **Connected** or **Not connected**. Until it is connected, use each board's application link instead.
- **LinkedIn**, **Indeed**, **ZipRecruiter** (the boards ticked for the role; ZipRecruiter only once you say in **Settings** that you have your own paid plan): ITS posts these by hand under your accounts, unless you chose in **Settings** that your team posts them. When ITS posts, each card says who at ITS has it; if ITS could not post it, the card says why, with **Ask ITS again**. Each card has that board's own application link with **Copy** and a visit count, **Copy posting package** (the title, employer, location, pay, description, screening questions and links, ready to paste), and the numbered steps. Once the job is up, ITS (or an owner or admin) pastes the posting's address under **Record the public address of the posting** and presses **Record the public URL**. The product opens that page and checks it shows your job, then the card says **Live and verified**. If the check fails, it tries twice more; after three failures the card says **Failed** and why. **Check again** repeats the check. **I checked it myself** lets a person confirm with a note of what they checked; that is recorded with their name.

Statuses you may see on a card: **Draft**, **Approved**, **Ready to post** (nothing is on the board yet), **Posted** (the address is recorded and being checked), **Submitted** (Google), **Live and verified**, **Paused**, **Failed**, **Closed**.

**Pausing or closing one board.** **Pause** and **Resume** on a card affect that posting only; resume puts it back where it was. **Close** ends it. The posting may still be visible on the board itself until someone takes it down there. When a board was up, the closed card shows **Removed from this board**: tick it once the posting is gone.

**Other links.** Make an application link for a referral, a direct approach or anything else: pick **Referral**, **Direct** or **Other**, give it a name, and press **Create link**. Applications through it carry that name. **Disable** stops a link; anyone who opens it then sees a not-found page. A board's own link cannot be disabled while its posting is up.

**The boards, the closing date and sharing.** **Where it is posted** has a tick for each board; a board you tick on an open role is prepared at once, and a board you untick is closed (and taken down if it was up). **Closing date** sets the last day new applications are taken, in your time zone: the job page then says "Apply by" that day, and at the end of it new applications stop on their own, with anyone part-way through the form still able to send it. Home then offers **Extend by two weeks**, **Choose another date** or **Close the role**. A Colorado or remote job always keeps a closing date (30 days out unless you choose another; the 30 days count from the day the job goes public, so from ITS's confirmation of your company if that comes later). A closing date picked in the job builder is saved with the draft, so it is there on another device too, and goes onto the job when it asks for approval. **Share it** gives each ready-made place its own link that opens the job page, so the applicant list says where each person came from.

**Applications by source (last 14 days).** How many people applied each day and through which board or link, counted in your time zone. If more than 100 people apply to a role in one day, owners and admins are told the next day. Nobody is turned away because of the number.

**Pause and Resume the role.** **Pause** in the job page's **More** menu stops new applications: the job page stays up and says the position is not accepting applications, the role disappears from your careers page and the job listings, and nobody is declined. **Resume** puts it back and adds any applications that arrived by email while it was paused. Pausing the role does not take the job off the boards; the Share tab reminds you of that.

**Stop accepting applications.** **Stop accepting applications** in the job page's **More** menu keeps the job listed and the page up, and says applications are closed. Nobody who already applied is affected, and nobody is declined. You can give a reason; your team is told. The banner under the title says since when. **Accept applications again** reopens it.

Only owners and admins can pause the role, stop accepting applications, or change postings and links. Hiring managers see the Share tab and ask an owner or admin, or ITS.

**Still up.** After a role closes, or after you close one board's posting, a red alert at the top of the role page and a card on **Home** name the boards where the job is still showing. They stay until each board is ticked **Removed from this board**.

**The Report tab.** The figures for the role, each with its definition at the bottom: who applied (by source and by day), where everyone is, how long each step takes, interviews, decisions waiting for you, what the assistant suggested and what you decided, when you overruled the system, recruiter help, messages to candidates (including any that could not be delivered), and outcomes. **Download the applicant list (CSV)** and **Download the summary (CSV)** give you spreadsheets; every download is recorded. Interviewers cannot open the report.

## 8. Bringing in a recruiter

Press **Bring in a recruiter** on Home, on a role, or on a candidate. In the panel:

1. From Home only: under **Which hire is this about?** choose one of your roles, or **All of your hiring**, and write one line under **In one line, what do you need?**. On a role or a candidate the request is about that role or candidate.
2. Pick what you want: **Review these candidates**, **Help me decide**, or **Ask ITS to run this hire** (an ITS recruiter contacts you to agree how they would run it; nothing changes until you have both agreed).
3. Pick **Normal** or **Urgent**. Urgent puts the request at the front of the ITS queue; it does not change the promised time.
4. On a role or a candidate, add anything specific under **Anything specific?** (optional).
5. Press **Send to a recruiter**.

Before you send, the panel says what the recruiter will see, the time they will respond by, and which of your plan's recruiter requests this one would be. You see "A recruiter has it", the expected response time and the access again.

**Your recruiter requests** on Home lists every request your team made, and those the product made for you, with its state, the reply-by time, who asked and who picked it up. When the recruiter answers, the person who asked and your owners are told, and the answer appears there in full (and on the candidate's timeline); it stays for two weeks, then folds under **Older requests**. A recruiter can also write to you before answering; you are told the same way. While a request is open, **Add a note for the recruiter** adds to it; the recruiter is told there is a note. Once it is answered, **This didn't solve it** opens it again: say what is still missing, and it gets a new reply-by time. It goes back to the recruiter who answered when they can still take it, otherwise to the ITS queue; the fresh access is recorded like any other and your team is told.

**What the recruiter can see.** Before accepting, only your company name, the role title, the kind of request, its urgency, the skill it needs and the time remaining. After accepting, what the request names: one candidate and the role they applied for, one role and its candidates, or all of your hiring when you chose that. Access lasts at most 14 days and ends a day after they mark it resolved. Every page they open is recorded in your **Access history** under **ITS recruiter access** in Settings, and you are told as soon as a recruiter starts.

**When your plan's roles are used up.** Your plan includes a number of roles at a time. A role uses one of them from when it is created (when someone asks for approval of a draft) while it is waiting for approval, sent back for changes, open or paused. Closing a role, filled or not, gives its place back straight away. Once they are all in use, drafting a new role says so before anything is typed, lists the roles using them (each opens its role, where you can close one you no longer need), and offers **Talk to ITS about more roles**. It opens the request form filled in, so you see what it shares before you send it. A talk about your plan does not use one of its recruiter requests.

**Recruiter requests and your plan.** Your plan includes a number of recruiter requests. Only the requests your own team makes count; going over never holds a request up, and ITS talks with you about the extra.

**Ending access.** In **Settings**, under **ITS recruiter access**, press **End this recruiter's access**, type a short reason under **Why you are ending it (the recruiter sees this)**, and press **End access now**. Access ends immediately, the recruiter is told, the request goes back to the queue, and your activity log records it. Only owners and admins can do this.

The product also brings in a recruiter on its own in three cases: a candidate asked for a person during a screen, a held application waited three days without a decision, or a chosen interview time collided three times. The recruiter then sees only that candidate or role, and you are told as soon as they start. A failed step that names no role or candidate goes to ITS administrators instead. You see these on Home like any other request. A recruiter working on one request can ask for more help only about the same candidate or role, never about the rest of your hiring, and you are told who asked when another recruiter picks it up.

## 9. Recording outcomes

The product never makes or sends an offer. When something happens outside the product, your team records it.

**An offer.** When your team has made a finalist an offer, they press **Offer made** on the candidate (or choose **Offer** on the stage button) and record the day. Nothing is sent to the candidate and no term of the offer is kept; the candidate shows as waiting on their answer, and you are reminded if none is recorded after five days. **They accepted** opens the hire sheet; **They declined** records it.

**A hire.** The hire sheet asks for the start date (and, optionally, the day they accepted), records the hire under your name and sends nothing to anyone. It then lists the other people still in the running and offers **Close it now** (the role, as filled: see section 10) or **Keep the role open**, in which case Home shows a "Keep your backups warm" card until the start date.

**Other outcomes.** Open the candidate in **Candidates** and choose **Record an outcome** from the page's menu. Under **Record what happened** choose:

- **They declined the offer made outside the platform**
- **The candidate withdrew**
- **We declined the candidate**, with a reason (only for people who may decline candidates; the step says what the candidate will get under your settings)
- **No hire for this candidate**

Press **Continue**, then **Yes, record it**. The record carries your name. Only an ITS administrator can correct it, and the original is kept. Nothing about an outcome is sent to the candidate; an interview they had booked or times they were offered are called off with a short note saying only that it is off.

**Team notes.** Each person on the hiring team can keep one private note on a candidate. Owners, admins, hiring managers, HR and an ITS recruiter you brought in read every note; an interviewer writes and reads only their own, on someone they interviewed. Notes are never shown to the candidate and never given to the AI assistant; a deletion request empties them.

**On a role.** See section 10.

## 10. Closing a role

On the job page open **More** and choose **Close the job**. Under **Close this job** choose the outcome:

- **Filled outside the platform**
- **Filled with a candidate from here (already recorded on the candidate)**
- **Cancelled**
- **Closed without a hire**

Add a reason if you like and press **Confirm and close the job**. Candidates who were still active are kept in your talent pool, privately, unless they asked not to be kept: nobody is told the decision until someone sends the message. If nobody tells them within 5 working days of closing, the closing message goes by itself (or, if your settings ask for approval or keep these messages with a person, an ITS recruiter is brought in instead and nothing is sent); the close card names the day. The close card then shows how many were still in process and one courteous closing message for all of them; one **Send** tells everyone (each once, with **Undo all** for 30 seconds), or one approval under Ask me first. A role closed from the hire sheet works the same way. Anyone with an interview booked or times offered gets a short note straight away saying only that the interview is off (no reason given). The role leaves your careers page and the job listings; the job page itself says the position is closed, and search engines drop it on their own. Every posting on the **Share** tab closes. The role then names the boards where the posting is still up, in a red alert at the top and on **Home**; ITS takes each one down and ticks **Removed from this board** on the Share tab.

Hiring managers can close a role too. Pausing a role and stopping applications are for owners and admins (section 7).

## 11. Privacy requests

Candidates can ask for a copy of their information, a correction, deletion, or a review by a person, from their status page or by email to privacy@identifiedtalent.com. Someone who has lost their status link can use the request form on the privacy page: we email them a confirmation link, and nothing is recorded until they press its button. A person at ITS handles every request, confirms receipt within one business day, and works to a tracked deadline.

**What you see.** That a request of a given kind exists and that it was completed. You do not see its content. A candidate who withdraws shows as withdrawn on their page and on Home.

**If a candidate writes to you directly** with a privacy request, forward it to privacy@identifiedtalent.com the same day and do not act on it yourself.

**If a candidate you hired asks for deletion**, ITS refers it to counsel, because employment records belong to you as the employer.

## 12. What "Development mode" means

You should never see the words "Development mode" or "fake provider" on your live workspace. They appear only on ITS's own test systems, where the AI is replaced by a stand-in and sign-in links are shown on screen. If you ever see a yellow banner reading "Development mode: the AI provider is a deterministic stand-in", or "Development mode" next to an invitation link, stop and email support@identifiedtalent.com. Do not enter real candidate information there.

## 13. Things that need ITS

Email support@identifiedtalent.com for any of these.

- Correcting a recorded outcome. An ITS administrator makes the correction and the original is kept.
- Changing your plan, your program dates or your allowances. An ITS administrator changes the plan in the ITS console with a written reason, which is recorded; what you used this month and the reset date are kept, and the new limits apply at once.
- Connecting incoming mail, so candidate replies and emailed applications come into the product.
- Connecting Google Calendar for your workspace.

ITS watches the service for you. A serious problem (for example the background worker stopping) raises an alert in the ITS console and is emailed to ITS administrators at once; an alert stays open until someone at ITS resolves it, and the same problem coming back raises it again. If the AI service stops answering for a while, applications wait and keep trying for over an hour, then are held (nothing is lost, and nobody is asked to review them by hand) until it answers again, when they carry on by themselves. If the email or text service is down, messages keep trying for about an hour before they count as not sent. Recruiter requests the product raises itself (a candidate asking for a person, a screening chat handed over, a scheduling problem) reach the ITS queue mailbox like the ones you raise, and one still unclaimed after its reply-by time raises an alert at ITS.

If ITS ever takes back its confirmation of your company, your job-board listings that were up are closed and marked to be taken down, so candidates do not meet dead links; the owners are told. Once ITS confirms again, post the boards again from the **Share** tab.

Everything else in this guide you can do yourself. ITS is glad to help with any of it: removing someone, changing a role, editing your company details or candidate notices, reading the report.
