# Hiring manager guide

**ITS AI Recruiting Department, Founding Partner Program. Release 1.**

Draft of 30 September 2026 (checked again against the product the same day), for review by ITS before it is given to a customer. This guide is for the person who runs a hire: describes the role, approves it, reads the candidates, and decides. Owners and admins can do everything in this guide too.

Screen names and button labels in **bold** are the ones you will see in the product. Two words we use: **ITS** is Identified Talent Solutions. **The assistant** is the software's AI helper. It organizes and drafts. You decide.

## 1. Your job in one paragraph

You describe who you need in a conversation. The assistant drafts a role packet. You read it, fix it and approve it. ITS posts the job. Candidates apply and answer a short written conversation with your AI assistant: in the same visit on the pilot roles ITS has switched on, by email afterwards otherwise (or they do it later, or ask for a person). The assistant reads each application and the answers, and shows you the evidence. You decide who to screen, interview and decline, and you record what happened. A decision is private: nothing that says no is sent to a candidate until someone on your team sends the decision message. Whenever you want a human recruiter's opinion, press **Bring in a recruiter**.

## 2. The job builder

Press **Start a hire** (in the menu on every page, or on **Today**), or type into the **Who are you hiring?** box on Today and press **Start drafting**. The job builder opens. Describe the role in a sentence or paste a job ad you already have (up to 20,000 characters), for example: "I need a dental hygienist for our Pasadena office, four days a week, $48 to $56 an hour, starting in November." At a desk, Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line; on a phone, press **Send**. If you have a draft in progress, **Start a hire** takes you back to it; **More**, then **Start a different role**, begins another.

The conversation is on the left and the draft on the right (on a phone, switch between **Conversation** and **Draft**). The assistant asks with question cards under the conversation: tap an answer (**Pick one** or **Pick any that apply**), or **Add your own…**, and press **Next**, then **Send** on the last card. **Why we ask** says why a question matters. Questions about people offer your team, plus **Someone not on the team yet**. **Or type your own answer…** brings the message box back. Your answers go into the conversation as one short message, and the draft on the right fills in, each part marked **Candidates see this**, **Candidates are asked this**, **Used to review applications; not on the job page** (the must-haves and nice-to-haves) or **Only your team sees this**. The **Pay** card shows the market pay for the job and place with its source and year (or says there is none), the range candidates see, and the most you would pay, which only your team sees.

The assistant always needs six things before the role can go for approval:

- the job title
- where the job is
- the pay range
- why you are hiring
- who will interview
- who makes the final decision

It may also ask about the schedule, the start date, must-haves and what you would like but do not require. Answer in plain words. If it asks something you have not decided, say so; it will note an assumption and you can fix it in the packet.

**What we know** and **Still missing** at the top of the draft list them as you go. When there is enough (or once you say "draft it"), press **Write the draft**; the steps show as they happen. Then type any change you want ("make it warmer", "drop the weekend requirement"): the change is shown first, field by field, and saved only when you press **Confirm** (**Keep as is** leaves the draft alone). **Edit** on the draft changes the answers as plain fields. The eye button shows the job exactly as candidates will see it on the job page.

You can write the draft before the pay is settled and set the pay afterwards on the **Pay** card; choosing who decides or who interviews later is fine too. Those go into the draft on their own. If you change other answers after the draft is written (the title, why you are hiring, the must-haves), the draft says so and offers **Write it again with them**. If you saved changes on the draft since it was written, it first tells you that writing it again replaces them.

**Ask for approval** stays greyed until everything needed is in and the draft is up to date with your answers; point at it to see what is missing. Pressing it creates the role from the draft as you see it, sends it to whoever approves it and takes you to the role page. Nothing is posted until it is approved. If you leave and come back, the draft is where you left it, and Today shows it as **Draft in progress**. **More**, then **Discard this draft**, sets it aside. If the assistant cannot help, **Bring in a recruiter** hands the draft and the conversation to ITS.

If you ask for something the law does not allow as a hiring criterion, the assistant declines it and suggests a lawful alternative. Wording in the draft that may stand in for a protected trait is flagged: change it, or keep it with a reason that whoever approves the role will see.

## 3. Reviewing and editing the role packet

The role page shows the title, location, type, work model and pay at the top, then these tabs.

**Overview.** The public job description, the pay (range, ceiling, whether it is shown to candidates, and where the numbers came from), why and when you are hiring, the schedule and work model (on site, hybrid or remote), **What matters to you** (the deal-breakers and what failure looks like, in your words from the conversation, and who makes the final decision), and where candidates will come from.

**Requirements.** The must-haves with the reason for each (at most six) and **Evidenced in**: where each one is checked (résumé, application, screen, interview; a must-have marked "screen" is asked about in the screening conversation), nice-to-haves, and **Eligibility questions (the only automatic holds)**. Candidates answer these when they apply. A "no" on a question marked **required** holds the application for your decision. Nothing is ever declined automatically. Below that, **Notices candidates see** lists the notices shown for the states involved.

**How we'll evaluate.** **What we look for** (the competencies, with a weight and where each is assessed), **Screening questions** with the time they take, the **Interview rounds** by name, and **How candidates are messaged**, the disclosure every message carries.

**Interview plan.** The rounds (one to five), each with its purpose, length and format, and who interviews in it. Next to each person: whether times can be offered to them yet (they have connected a calendar or declared their hours) or not. **Assign interviewers** (or **Change**) picks people from your team for that round; saving is a new packet version called "Interviewers assigned". Assign people before the first interview is approved, so times can go out at once.

**Posting.** Where the job will appear once approved (section 5).

**Activity.** Everything that happened on the role, newest first, in plain words: drafted, approved, opened, posted, applications arriving, decisions. Candidates are not named here; Talent has the people.

**History.** Every version of the packet, who made it and why. Nothing is overwritten.

**Report.** The role's figures once people apply (section 16).

**To edit**, press **Edit** (or **Edit first** on the approval card). The editor lets you change:

- **The job**: **Job title**, the places (**City** and **State**; **Add a place**), **Full or part time**, **Work model** and **Schedule**
- **Public description**
- **Pay**: **Paid** (by the hour or a yearly salary), **Pay minimum** and **Pay maximum**, **Ceiling (the most you would pay)**: offers never go above this without a new approval, and **Benefits**
- **Must-haves (up to six)**: one box per must-have with **What they must bring**, **Why it matters**, and **Where we check it** (résumé, application, screen, interview; pick at least one). **Add a must-have** adds a box; **Remove** takes one away. Existing must-haves keep their identity, so earlier assessments still line up
- **Nice-to-haves (up to ten)**: the same boxes; never a reason to hold anyone
- **Screening questions (3 to 12)**: reword, choose what each is about, move up or down, remove or add
- **Questions on the application form**: only the kinds that may hold an application (a licence, a certification, work authorization, the schedule, the start date, getting to the workplace, a genuine physical requirement, a legal minimum age), each with why it is asked
- **Deal-breakers, in your words**, **Who has the final say** (someone on your team, or someone not on it yet) and the **Tone of messages to candidates**

Write a line under **What changed and why** and press **Save as a new version**. The old version stays in History. On the **Overview** tab, **Change it by asking** takes a change in plain words and shows it before saving, and lists suggestions about the requirements with one-tap fixes. **Candidate view** shows the job as candidates will see it. Admins and ITS recruiters also see **Everything (advanced)**, the whole packet for someone technical. If the editor refuses a save, tell ITS and we make the change with you. A filled or closed role offers **Hire for this role again**, which starts a new draft from it and asks you to confirm the pay again.

Things to check before you approve:

- Is the pay range one you would actually pay, and is the ceiling right?
- Are the must-haves really must-haves? Each one becomes something the assistant looks for evidence of. Six is the maximum; three or four is often better.
- Are the eligibility questions lawful and necessary? They are the only questions that can hold an application.
- Do the interview rounds match how you actually interview, and are the right people named?
- Read the public description as a candidate would.

## 4. Approval

The card **Approve this role packet** lists any assumptions the assistant made and says who can decide. **Preview the job page** opens it exactly as candidates will see it, in a new tab (never public). The card shows what the job page lists of your must-haves and nice-to-haves (**Candidates see this**; why each matters stays with your team), which job boards it goes to, anything the law where the job is still asks of the ad (approving waits for it), and what starts on its own once the job is live. Press **Approve and publish**. The role becomes **Open**, the careers page and job page go live, and a short screen shows where the job is going, each place ticking only once it was checked. While ITS has not yet made its one-time check of your company, the card says so: the role opens here but nothing of it is public, the role page and its Posting tab say why the job page shows "not found" for now, and it goes public on its own as soon as ITS confirms your company. Owners, admins and hiring managers can approve, with two rules: you cannot approve a role you started or a version you wrote yourself, unless you are the company's only owner (the card tells you so); and if the packet names a decision maker who is on your team, the approval is theirs, though an owner can also decide. Sending a packet back follows the same rule: the named decision maker, or an owner.

If the card lists wording to check (a phrase that may stand in for a protected trait, or a pay figure in the description that is not the range set for the job), the role cannot be approved until each one is dealt with: press **Change the wording** to edit it, or **Keep it…** and say why. Whoever approves sees who kept it and the reason. Sending the role back is always possible, whatever the card lists.

If something should change first, press **Send back with a note** and say what. The role becomes a draft again and its page shows **Sent back** with your note. Whoever edits it presses **Request approval again** when it is ready; every edit is kept as a version. An approval nobody decides within 14 days goes back to a draft the same way.

Editing after approval creates a new version. If you can approve, your edit takes effect at once; if you can edit but not approve (a recruiter, for instance), the edit waits on the approval card as "Approve packet v3 of ..." while the role keeps running on its current version. Candidates who already applied are assessed against the version they applied to.

## 5. The Posting tab and the manual posting steps

One card per place the job appears, each with a status and a sentence saying what happens next. Everyone who works on the role can read this tab. The buttons that change a posting, pause the role or stop applications are for owners and admins (and ITS); if you are a hiring manager and something needs changing, ask your owner or your ITS contact.

- **Careers page**: your job page, served by the product. The address is on the card with **Copy**. The product opens the page and checks it before calling it **Live and verified**.
- **Google for Jobs**: the job page carries the data Google reads. It shows **Submitted** until someone sees the job in Google and marks it verified with a note.
- **Applications by email**: an address for forwarded applications. It works once ITS has connected incoming mail for your workspace; until then the card says **Not connected** and the boards use their own application links.
- **LinkedIn**, **Indeed**, **ZipRecruiter** (the boards ticked for the role): these are posted by ITS by hand under your company's accounts, unless your company chose to post them itself. Here is what happens:
  1. The card shows **Ready to post**, with the steps, **Copy posting package** (everything the board asks for, ready to paste) and the board's own application link. Each board has a different link, so the product knows where each applicant came from.
  2. ITS posts the job on the board with that package and that link. The card says who at ITS has it. If ITS cannot post it, the card says why, and an owner or admin can press **Ask ITS again** or post it from the company's own account.
  3. ITS records the public address of the posting. The card changes to **Posted**.
  4. The product opens the address and checks that it shows your job. If it cannot, it tries twice more, then the card says **Failed** and why; **Check again** repeats it, and a person who can see the posting can press **I checked it myself** and say what they checked.
  5. The card changes to **Live and verified**, with who or what verified it and when. Nothing is called live before this.

When ITS posts, you do not have to do anything on the boards yourself. If a board asks your account holder to confirm something, ITS tells you. After an approved change, a board that still shows the earlier version says so until it is updated. Paid promotion on a board is bought by you, from the board.

**Pausing or closing one board.** **Pause** on a card pauses that posting only, and **Resume** puts it back where it was. **Close** ends it. The job may still show on the board itself until someone takes it down there.

**Still up.** When the role closes, or when one board's posting is closed, the role page shows a red alert naming the boards where the job is still showing. When ITS posts for your company, ITS gets a task to take each one down; when your team posts itself, owners and admins get a card on **Today**. Once the posting is gone from a board, an owner or admin (or ITS) ticks **Removed from this board** on its card. The alert stays until every board is ticked.

**Other links.** Under **Other links** you can make an application link for a referral, a direct approach or anything else. Applications through it carry the name you gave it.

**Applications by source (last 14 days).** How many people applied each day, and through which board or link, in your time zone. If more than 100 people apply in a day, owners and admins are told the next day. Nobody is ever turned away because of the number.

**Pause** at the top of the role page stops new applications without declining anyone: the job page says the position is not accepting applications and the role disappears from your careers page. **Resume** puts it back. If you have enough applicants and want the job to stay listed but closed, use **Stop accepting applications** at the top of the role page instead: the page stays up and says applications are closed, a banner under the title says since when, nobody who applied is affected, and nobody is declined. **Accept applications again** reopens it. Either way, the board postings stay up until they are paused or removed there; tell your ITS contact.

## 6. The Talent view

**Talent** shows the candidates for one role. If you have more than one role, pick it at the top right. The line under the title says what the page is for: who applied, what the evidence says, and what needs you. Nobody is declined automatically on judgment.

**Groups.** Five tabs across the top: **All**, **Waiting on you**, **In process**, **Not moving forward**, **Hired**. Start with **Waiting on you**. Next to them, **Needs a closer look** narrows the list to the candidates the assistant could not say enough about (the number in brackets is how many).

**The card.** Each candidate shows their name, where they applied from, their stage in plain words ("Just applied", "Ready for your review", "Invited to a screen", "Interviewing", "Finalist", "Not moving forward" and so on), their most recent positions, years of experience, licenses, where they are based, and any flags on the resume. If an eligibility answer did not match, it says **Did not meet** and the question. If the assistant has written an assessment, the card shows the overall reading, the confidence and a one-line summary, then its top strength (with where the evidence came from), its top concern, and how many questions are still open ("2 questions open").

**The overall readings** are **Strong match**, **Potential match**, **Weak match** and **Insufficient evidence**. They are not scores. They summarize what the evidence shows against your must-haves and what you look for.

**Needs a closer look.** When the assistant could not say enough (the reading is Insufficient evidence, or it recommends a person look), the candidate stays in **Waiting on you** with the label **Needs a closer look** and the card says what is missing in plain words: the open questions, or that the resume could not be read. The notification you receive says the same. Two things can be missing that are worth knowing: **The résumé could not be read; the written answers were used** means the file was fine but had no text in it (a photo or a scan), so the assessment rests on what the candidate typed; **We could not assess this application automatically; please review it yourself** means the assistant's answer was unusable twice, so there is no assessment at all. Nothing is hidden in either case: the resume, the answers and the messages are on the candidate's page. The button on the card reads **Take a closer look**.

Press **Review** (or the name) to open the candidate.

**Comparing candidates.** When a role has more than one candidate, press **Compare** at the top of Talent. Tick up to five people and press **Compare selected**. A table puts them side by side: where each person is, then one row per must-have (met, partly met, not met, unknown) and per thing you look for (seen, partly seen, not seen yet, contradicted), then the screen's recommendation, the resume reading, and how much is still unknown. "No evidence yet" means nothing has spoken to that row. The columns are in the order you ticked them. Nothing is ranked or scored; the page says so. Press **Done comparing** to go back to the list.

**Inside the candidate page**, from top to bottom:

- A card for anything the assistant proposed and is waiting on you for, such as **The assistant suggests a screen** with **Yes, invite to a screen**.
- **Interviews**: each round, its status and the decision.
- **How did the interview go?** when an interview is over and no decision is recorded (section 12).
- **What would you like to do?** with the buttons **Invite to a screen**, **Interview**, **Hold**, **Decline** and **Bring in a recruiter**. Buttons that do not apply at this stage are greyed out; hover to see why.
- **Record what happened** (section 14).
- **Held on an eligibility answer** or **Declined on an eligibility answer**, when that applies (section 7).
- **Message (first name)**: write to the candidate in your own words (section 15).
- **Contact and consent**: email, phone, location, text-message consent (off in release 1) and when the AI notice was shown.
- **Screening conversation**: the status and, when there is one, **Read the conversation**.
- **Resume assessment** and, after a screen, **Screen scorecard** (section 9).
- **What the resume says**: the positions with dates and a quoted line from the resume for each, licenses, skills, and any flags. Below, **Open (file name)** opens the actual resume; the opening is recorded. A file that is still being checked says so; a refused file says why.
- **Eligibility answers**: which required answers were met.
- **Messages**: everything sent to the candidate and whether it arrived, and anything they wrote back, including email replies once ITS has connected incoming mail.
- **What happened**: the full timeline with who did what.

**What the assistant recommends, and what it never does.** The assistant recommends: it may say a candidate is worth a screen, worth an interview, worth a hold, or that it has a concern. It never declines anyone. It never ranks candidates against each other. It never sends a decline message. If it cannot produce an assessment, you see the application without one. If an assessment looks wrong, use **Something wrong?** on it (section 9).

## 7. Held on an eligibility answer

When a candidate answers no to a required eligibility question, the card reads **Held on an eligibility answer** with the question and the answer. Nothing is sent to the candidate. You are reminded daily, and an ITS recruiter is brought in after three days if nobody decides.

- If the answer was a mistake or you want to keep them anyway, type why under **Why keep them in?** and press **Keep them in**. Your reason is recorded.
- If you agree they do not meet the requirement, press **Decline now**, read what follows, and press **Confirm decline**. Nothing happens on the first press. The decision is private: nothing is sent to the candidate until someone sends the decision message (section 13), which then explains it was their eligibility answer.

A candidate declined this way shows **Declined on an eligibility answer** with a **Review anyway** button in case you change your mind.

## 8. The screening conversation

**In the same visit as applying (pilot roles that ITS has switched on).** Everyone who passes your eligibility questions goes straight from the application form to a short written conversation with your AI assistant. Nobody has to invite them. While they are in it they show apart, under **Still applying**, and you are not alerted about them until they finish, send their application without it, or stop part-way.

What the candidate sees, on a phone or a computer, with no app and no password:

- First, a short introduction: it is your company's AI assistant, a software program, not a person; it is a conversation, not a test; about how many questions and minutes (five or six questions, about ten minutes, is the usual length); they can skip a question or stop and come back. Three buttons: **Start**, **Do this later** (the link goes to them by email) and **I'd rather talk to a person**. Every page of the conversation says at the top that it is the AI assistant.
- Then one question at a time, in your packet's own words, with "Question 2 of 5" and a thin progress bar. They answer in their own words; for simple facts (yes or no, which days, a start date, the pay they are looking for) they can tap an answer instead. The assistant may ask a short follow-up that quotes what they said. **Skip this question** is always there; a skipped question counts as unknown, never against them.
- At the end, **Check your answers**: every answer with **Change**, then **Send my answers**. You hear about them only after they press Send.

**I'd rather talk to a person** gives the candidate two equal choices: **Send without the conversation** (their application comes to you as it is) or **Have a person contact me** (an ITS recruiter gets in touch within four business hours; ITS covers it, and it does not use your plan's recruiter requests). Both are counted on the role's figures.

If they stop part-way, they get one reminder the next day; two days after that the conversation closes as unfinished, what they said comes to you marked "not finished", and they are told nothing else is needed. If they answered everything but did not press Send, the reminder asks them to check and send, and two days later their answers come to you as they are, read like anyone's. If they never start it, they get one reminder after two days; three days after that their application comes to you without it, and they are told that is fine. If the assistant stops working, the conversation pauses with their answers kept and they are emailed a link to finish once it works again; after a day without it, a person takes over and they are told so. A person is always one click away.

Each conversation counts against your plan's monthly allowance at the candidate's first answer, never at the invitation. At 80% you and ITS are warned. If the allowance runs out, applications keep coming in, and people who apply meanwhile get the link by email once more conversations are added, so everyone for a role has the same process.

**Later, by email (every role ITS has not switched to the same visit, and any role whose screen invitations are "Ask me first" or "Only a person").** Candidates send the form only. Under the default rule the assistant invites candidates whose applications look like a fit and tells you under **What the assistant did**; you can also invite anyone yourself: open the candidate and press **Invite to a screen**. With "Ask me first" you see **The assistant suggests a screen** and press **Yes, invite to a screen**. The candidate gets an email with the link; the page opens on the same introduction, and the conversation works as above. If they have not started after two days they get one reminder. If they have not started three days after that, the invitation lapses, the candidate shows "No response", and you are told. You can then decline with a reason, keep them, or ask a recruiter to call.

## 9. Reading a scorecard

When the screen is complete, the candidate's page gets a **Screen scorecard** and the candidate moves to **Waiting on you**. It has:

- three chips: the overall reading, the recommendation (**Worth a screen**, **Hold**, **Needs a closer look**, **Strong: interview**, **Interview** or **Concern raised**), and the confidence
- a summary, and under it why the confidence is what it is ("Confidence is low because no résumé, only the written summary; two must-haves have no evidence")
- **Pay expectation** and **Availability** if the candidate stated them, with whether they fit your range and schedule
- **Must-haves**: each one with yes, partially, no or unknown, and the quoted evidence
- **Answers**: what each screening answer showed
- **Competencies**: what was evidenced, partially evidenced, not evidenced or contradicted
- **Strengths** and **Concerns**, each with its source and a quote
- **Unknown**: what could not be established and why it matters
- flags, which are never used to decline, and the assistant's reasoning

The **Resume assessment**, written when the application arrived, has the same shape without the answers.

Read the evidence, not just the chips. Unknowns are questions to ask; under them, **Ask about this in a screen** invites the candidate to a short web screen that starts with exactly those questions before the usual ones (the invitation says so: "a few quick questions about your background"). The answers appear on the candidate's page beside the questions. When a screen is already open the button gives way to a sentence saying so, since the invitation cannot be sent twice. If something is wrong, use **Something wrong?** on the card and pick the reason: **Cited the wrong evidence**, **Made something up**, **Broke a rule we set**, **Bias concern**, **Poorly worded**, **Took the wrong next step**, **Missed a good candidate**, **Overrated this candidate** or **Missed context we gave**. A person at ITS reviews every flag. Flagging changes nothing about the candidate.

## 10. Approving an interview and picking interviewers

Nothing schedules until a person approves it. Open the candidate and press **Interview**. If the assistant proposed it, you see **The assistant suggests an interview** and press **Yes, interview** first.

Under **Approve an interview**:

1. **Round**: the next round from the interview plan is selected. Change it if needed. A round must be in the approved plan.
2. **Interviewers**: tick the people. Each shows **availability known** or **no calendar or hours yet**. Someone with no availability cannot be offered times; they will be asked to set it in Setup, and scheduling waits for them.
3. **Meeting link**: **Create it with the calendar event**, or **I'll paste a link** and paste your own video or phone details.
4. Press **Approve and offer times**.

Times are worked out from each interviewer's connected calendar or declared hours, within a scheduling window, on business days, respecting the buffer and daily maximum. If the interviewers have no common free time, you are told and can widen the window, change interviewers or free some time.

## 11. The candidate picking a time

The candidate gets an email with a link to "Pick a time for your (round)". They see the times in their own time zone (they can change it), pick one, can add a note under "Anything that would make the interview easier for you?", and press **Confirm this time**. If none work they press **Ask for other times** with a note; you are told and new times go out when the window or availability changes.

When they confirm, the product books the time on every connected calendar, sends the candidate a confirmation with a calendar file, tells the interviewers by email and on Today, and moves the candidate to "Interviewing". If a chosen time was taken in the meantime, new times go out on their own; after three collisions an ITS recruiter arranges it directly.

The candidate can **Ask for a different time** twice on their own and can **Cancel the interview**. Either way you are told and nothing happens automatically after a cancellation.

If the candidate does not pick a time: a reminder after two days, and after four days the times expire and you are told to offer new times, decide, or bring in a recruiter.

Reminders go to the candidate and the interviewers one day before and two hours before.

**On the Interviews page** you see every interview **In motion** (Preparing times, Waiting on the candidate, Scheduled) and **Past**, with the interviewer load. Open one to see details, the times offered, an accommodation request if any, and to change it:

- **Book this for them**, next to an offered time, if you agreed a time by phone. It is recorded as your action.
- **Offer new times** to reschedule from your side.
- **Cancel interview** with a reason the candidate sees, then **Confirm cancel**. The candidate and interviewers are told.
- **Message the candidate** opens the candidate's page with the message box ready (section 15).
- After the interview, **After the interview** shows the decision, or **Record the decision**, which takes you to the candidate's page (section 12).

Interviewers receive the booking, the reminders and the link, and a brief: in the confirmation email and on the interview page under **Your brief**. It says what the round is for, what to look for, the questions the plan suggests, how the candidate stands on your must-haves, strengths so far, what is worth exploring, and what they said in the screen. Interviewers never see pay. They can open only the candidates they interview, and they do not see the Talent list.

## 12. Recording the decision after an interview

After the interview, the interviewers (and anyone who manages interviews) see **Did it take place?** on the interview page: **Yes**, **Candidate did not show**, or **We did not show**. Answer it; it keeps the record straight. A no-show is never held against anyone automatically.

Once the interview is over, the candidate waits on you: they appear under **Waiting on you** in Talent, the people who decide get a notification ("How did it go with ...?") that opens the candidate's page, and **Today** shows a card. On the candidate's page you see **How did the interview go?**. The decision is yours and is recorded with your name; nothing here is scored by software.

- **Next round**: the candidate moves on. The page opens the interview approval for the next round with its interviewers; check them and approve, and times go out from there.
- **Finalist**: write what stood out under **What stood out (kept internally)** and press **Confirm finalist**.
- **Not moving forward**: pick a reason, add a note if you like, and press **Confirm: not moving forward**. The decision is private: nothing reaches the candidate until someone sends the decision message (section 13).

Release 1 has no interviewer scorecards. Record what matters in the note. The decision shows on the interview page too.

Owners, admins and hiring managers record these decisions (and an ITS recruiter you brought in). HR and interviewers can say whether an interview took place but cannot decide.

## 13. Declining with a reason

At any stage, open the candidate, press the stage button and choose **Not moving forward**. (Someone held on an eligibility answer is declined from that answer's own card instead, section 7.) A caution reminds you that declining is your decision and is recorded with your reason. Pick a **Reason**:

- **Experience isn't the right fit**
- **Missing skills we need**
- **Pay expectations don't match**
- **Location or schedule**
- **Position filled**
- **Other**

Add a **Note (kept internally)** if you like and press **Confirm: not moving forward**. The decision is private: it is not sent to the candidate, and their status page still shows the step they were at. If they had an interview booked or times offered, it is cancelled now, and they get a short note straight away saying only that the interview is off (no reason given), so nobody turns up to an interview that no longer exists. The confirmation names that interview, with its day and time, before you press the button. The decision message you send later does not mention the interview again. Declined candidates appear under **Not moving forward** with **Not told yet**, and the reason is on their timeline. A candidate is never declined by software.

**Telling the candidate.** Telling is a separate step, by a person. On the candidate's page, **Send message** shows the exact standard message first; **Send** sends it (under **Ask me first** it waits for a second person to approve it; under **Only a person** the product sends no decision messages). To tell several people at once, use **Not told yet** on the Talent page, then **Review and send**: each person gets their own message, and you see what happened for each. If you told them yourself (by phone, say), press **I told them myself** and nothing more is sent. Only once they were told does their status page show the decision. Anyone not told after two working days is listed on **Today** and you get a reminder; nothing goes out by itself.

**On hold** keeps a candidate where they are while you think, until a date you choose (two weeks unless you change it); they come back to **Today** then. It sends nothing.

## 14. Recording the outcome

The product never makes or sends an offer, and it asks for no pay, tax, identity or banking details. When you have made an offer your own way, record what happened. On the candidate's page under **Record what happened**, press **Record an outcome** and choose:

- **Hired (offer accepted outside the platform)**: add the hire date, and the date the offer was accepted if you know it
- **Declined an offer made outside the platform**
- **The candidate withdrew**
- **We declined the candidate**: add a reason. Like any decline it is private until someone sends the decision message (section 13). Shown only to people who may decline candidates.
- **No hire for this candidate**

Press **Continue**, then **Yes, record it**. The record carries your name and can only be corrected by an ITS administrator, with the original kept. After a hire, the product asks **Close the role too?**. Closing the role is a separate decision, made from the role page, and candidates who were still active stay in your talent pool, privately. Anyone among them with an interview booked or times offered gets a short note straight away saying only that the interview is off.

## 15. Writing to a candidate

On the candidate's page, the **Message (first name)** card has **Write to (first name)**. (From an interview, **Message the candidate** opens it for you.) Add a subject if you like, write your message, and press **Send**.

- It goes out under your company's hiring-team name, with your name at the bottom. It is your own words, so it carries no AI notice. The card shows who it goes to and the footer before you send.
- Under the default settings it goes at once and the owners are told. If your owner has set **Send a message a team member wrote to a candidate** to Ask me first or Only a person, the button says **Send for approval**: an owner or admin reads it first, on **Today** or on the candidate's page, and presses **Yes, send it** or **Do not send**. You are told either way. Messages from owners and admins always go at once.
- A candidate with no email address on file cannot be written to; the card says so.
- Replies: once ITS has connected incoming mail for your workspace, the candidate's reply appears under **Messages** on their page and you are told. Until then, replies reach an ITS mailbox that a person reads. The card tells you which.
- The message itself is on the candidate's page. The audit history records who wrote it and who approved it, never the text.

## 16. The report

The **Report** tab on the role page shows the role's figures, each defined at the bottom of the tab:

- **Who applied**: by source (careers page, each board's link, referral links, email) and by day.
- **Where everyone is**: how many people are at each step, grouped as waiting on you, in process, not moving forward and hired.
- **How long each step takes**: the typical and average days, and the longest anyone has waited.
- **Interviews**: requested, on the calendar, held, missed, cancelled, rescheduled by the candidate, and waiting for your decision.
- **Decisions waiting for you**, **What the assistant suggested, and what you decided**, **When you overruled the system**.
- **Recruiter help**, **Messages to candidates** (including any that could not be delivered, so you can reach those people another way), and **Outcomes**.

**Download the applicant list (CSV)** and **Download the summary (CSV)** give you spreadsheets. Every download is recorded. Interviewers cannot open the report.

## 17. When to bring in a recruiter

Press **Bring in a recruiter** whenever you want a human opinion. Good moments:

- You have several plausible candidates and want a recruiter to review them before you decide.
- You are unsure whether to interview someone.
- You do not have time to run the role and would like ITS to run the hire for you.
- A candidate cannot be reached, or something about a candidate needs a person.

Choose **Review these candidates**, **Help me decide** or **Ask ITS to run this hire**, add anything specific, and press **Send to a recruiter**. Asking ITS to run the hire starts a conversation: an ITS recruiter contacts you to agree how they would run it, and nothing changes until you have both agreed. Before you send, the form tells you what the recruiter will see (one candidate and their role, one role and its candidates, or all of your hiring), the time they will reply by, and which of your plan's recruiter requests this one would be. **Urgent** puts the request at the front of the ITS queue; the reply-by time stays the same.

The recruiter sees what the request covers: the role, its candidates and their messages and decisions so far, or one candidate. Their access starts when they pick the request up, ends a day after it is answered or after 14 days at most, and every page they open is recorded; an owner or admin can end it at any time in **Setup**. You are told when a recruiter picks your request up.

Your requests are listed on **Today** under **Your recruiter requests**, with their state, the reply-by time and who picked each one up. The recruiter's answer appears there in full, and you are told when it comes in; it stays on Today for two weeks, and older requests fold under **Older requests**. While a request is open you can press **Add a note for the recruiter**. Once it is answered, if the answer did not solve it, press **This didn't solve it** and say what is still missing: the request opens again with a new reply-by time. It goes back to the recruiter who answered when they can still take it, otherwise to the ITS queue, and whoever has it gets fresh access, which your team is told about and which is recorded like any other.
