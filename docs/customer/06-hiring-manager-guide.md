# Hiring manager guide

**ITS AI Recruiting Department, Founding Partner Program. Release 1.**

Draft of 30 September 2026, brought up to date with the product on 15 October 2026, for review by ITS before it is given to a customer. This guide is for the person who runs a hire: describes the role, approves it, reads the candidates, and decides. Owners and admins can do everything in this guide too.

Screen names and button labels in **bold** are the ones you will see in the product. Two words we use: **ITS** is Identified Talent Solutions. **The assistant** is the software's AI helper. It organizes and drafts. You decide.

## 1. Your job in one paragraph

You describe who you need in a conversation. The assistant drafts a role packet. You read it, fix it and approve it. ITS posts the job. Candidates apply and answer a short written conversation with your AI assistant: in the same visit on the pilot roles ITS has switched on, by email afterwards otherwise (or they do it later, or ask for a person). The assistant reads each application and the answers, and shows you the evidence. You decide who to screen, interview and decline, and you record what happened. A decision is private: nothing that says no is sent to a candidate until someone on your team sends the decision message. Whenever you want a human recruiter's opinion, press **Bring in a recruiter**.

## 2. The job builder

Press **Start a hire** (in the menu on every page, or on **Home**). The first page, **Who are you hiring?**, offers two ways to start.

**Answer a few quick questions** (the main button, **Start the quick questions**) asks ten short questions, one per screen, with "Step N of 10": the job title (or paste a job ad you already have, and the answers are filled in from it), the employment type, where the job is, the minimum experience (and whether someone with less is still considered), the pay (always shown on the job ad), the closing date (or **No closing date**), the must-have skills (suggestions for that job to tick, plus your own; at least one, at most six), the deal-breakers (or **None**; each one is shown openly as a must-have unless you untick it), and the screening questions (suggested yes or no questions, already ticked; untick or add your own). For a job our hiring guides do not cover, the AI suggests skills and questions from your answers so far, marked **AI suggestion**: the skills are never ticked for you, and the questions are ticked only if they arrived while you were on that screen. While it works the screen says "Suggesting skills from your answers…"; if it cannot help, you simply add your own. Only the job's facts are sent to it, and every suggestion passes the same fairness check. The last screen lists every answer with **Edit**. Enter moves on. Your answers are kept in this browser if you leave and come back; **Start over** clears them. **Write the job post** hands everything to the job builder below, which writes the draft at once. Every must-have, deal-breaker and question gets the same fairness check as in the conversation, and anything refused is listed with the reason. The assistant still asks what the questions did not cover, such as why you are hiring and who interviews and decides.

**Or describe it, or paste a job ad.** Type into the box on that page, or into the **Who are you hiring?** box on Home and press **Start drafting**. The job builder opens. Describe the role in a sentence or paste a job ad you already have (up to 20,000 characters), for example: "I need a dental hygienist for our Pasadena office, four days a week, $48 to $56 an hour, starting in November." At a desk, Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line; on a phone, press **Send**. If you have a draft in progress, **Start a hire** takes you back to it; **More**, then **Start a different role**, begins another.

The conversation is on the left and the draft on the right (on a phone, switch between **Conversation** and **Draft**). The assistant asks with question cards under the conversation: tap an answer (**Pick one** or **Pick any that apply**), or **Add your own…**, and press **Next**, then **Send** on the last card. **Why we ask** says why a question matters. Questions about people offer your team, plus **Someone not on the team yet**. **Or type your own answer…** brings the message box back. Your answers go into the conversation as one short message, and the draft on the right fills in, each part marked **Candidates see this**, **Candidates are asked this**, **Used to review applications; not on the job page** (the must-haves and nice-to-haves) or **Only your team sees this**. The **Pay** card shows the market pay for the job and place with its source and year (or says there is none), the range candidates see, and the most you would pay, which only your team sees.

The assistant always needs six things before the role can go for approval:

- the job title
- where the job is
- the pay range
- why you are hiring
- who will interview
- who makes the final decision

It may also ask about the schedule, the start date, must-haves and what you would like but do not require. Answer in plain words. If it asks something you have not decided, say so; it will note an assumption and you can fix it in the packet.

**What we know** and **Still missing** at the top of the draft list them as you go. When there is enough (or once you say "draft it"), press **Write the draft**; the steps show as they happen. Then type any change you want ("make it warmer", "drop the weekend requirement"): the change is shown first, field by field, and saved only when you press **Confirm** (**Keep as is** leaves the draft alone). **Edit** on the draft changes the answers as plain fields. The eye button shows the job exactly as candidates will see it on the job page.

You can write the draft before the pay is settled and set the pay afterwards on the **Pay** card; choosing who decides or who interviews later is fine too. Those go into the draft on their own. If you change other answers after the draft is written (the title, why you are hiring, the must-haves), the draft says so and offers **Write it again with them**. If you saved changes on the draft since it was written, it first tells you that writing it again replaces them.

**Ask for approval** stays greyed until everything needed is in and the draft is up to date with your answers; point at it to see what is missing. Pressing it creates the role from the draft as you see it, sends it to whoever approves it and takes you to the role page. Nothing is posted until it is approved. If you leave and come back, the draft is where you left it, and Home shows it as **Draft in progress**. **More**, then **Discard this draft**, sets it aside. If the assistant cannot help, **Bring in a recruiter** hands the draft and the conversation to ITS.

If you ask for something the law does not allow as a hiring criterion, the assistant declines it and suggests a lawful alternative. Wording in the draft that may stand in for a protected trait is flagged: change it, or keep it with a reason that whoever approves the role will see.

## 3. Reviewing and editing the role packet

The job page shows the title, its status and one line of facts (place, type, pay) at the top, with **Bring in a recruiter** and a **More** menu (Edit, Candidate view, Pause or Resume, Stop accepting applications, Close the job). Then one tab strip, the same on the job's candidates: **Candidates** (with how many applied; section 6), **Job**, **Scoring**, **Interview plan**, **Share**, **Report** and **Activity**. Older links to the Overview, Requirements, Posting or History tab open the tab that now holds it.

**Job.** The public job description, the pay (range, ceiling, whether it is shown to candidates, and where the numbers came from), why and when you are hiring, the schedule and work model (on site, hybrid or remote), **What matters to you** (the deal-breakers and what failure looks like, in your words from the conversation, and who makes the final decision), and where candidates will come from.

**Scoring: the requirements.** The must-haves with the reason for each (at most six) and **Evidenced in**: where each one is checked (résumé, application, screen, interview; a must-have marked "screen" is asked about in the screening conversation), nice-to-haves, and **Eligibility questions (the only automatic holds)**. Candidates answer these when they apply. A "no" on a question marked **required** holds the application for your decision. Nothing is ever declined automatically. Below that, **Notices candidates see** lists the notices shown for the states involved.

**Scoring: how we'll evaluate.** **What we look for** (the competencies, with a weight and where each is assessed), **Screening questions** with the time they take, the **Interview rounds** by name, and **How candidates are messaged**, the disclosure every message carries.

**Interview plan.** The rounds (one to five), each with its purpose, length and format, and who interviews in it. Next to each person: whether times can be offered to them yet (they have connected a calendar or declared their hours) or not. **Assign interviewers** (or **Change**) picks people from your team for that round; saving is a new packet version called "Interviewers assigned". Assign people before the first interview is approved, so times can go out at once.

**Share.** The job's public link to copy or open, the **Post on LinkedIn** card, then where the job will appear once approved (section 5).

**Activity.** Everything that happened on the job, newest first, in plain words: drafted, approved, opened, posted, applications arriving, decisions; and every version of the job details. Candidates are not named here; the **Candidates** tab has the people.

**History.** Every version of the packet, who made it and why. Nothing is overwritten.

**Report.** The role's figures once people apply (section 16).

**To edit**, press **Edit** (or **Edit first** on the approval card). The editor lets you change:

- **The job**: **Job title**, the places (**City** and **State**; **Add a place**), **Full or part time**, **Work model** and **Schedule**
- **Public description**
- **Pay**: **Paid** (by the hour or a yearly salary), **Pay minimum** and **Pay maximum**, **Ceiling (the most you would pay)**: offers never go above this without a new approval, and **Benefits**
- **Must-haves (up to six)**: one box per must-have with **What they must bring**, **Why it matters**, and **Where we check it** (résumé, application, screen, interview; pick at least one). **Add a must-have** adds a box; **Remove** takes one away. Existing must-haves keep their identity, so earlier assessments still line up
- **Nice-to-haves (up to ten)**: the same boxes; never a reason to hold anyone
- **Screening questions (3 to 12)**: reword, choose what each is about, move up or down, remove or add
- **Questions on the application form**: only the kinds that may hold an application (a licence, a certification, work authorization, the schedule, the start date, getting to the workplace, a genuine physical requirement, a legal minimum age), each with why it is asked
- **Deal-breakers, in your words**, **Who has the final say** (someone on your team, or someone not on it yet) and the **Tone of messages to candidates**

Write a line under **What changed and why** and press **Save as a new version**. The old version stays in History. On the **Overview** tab, **Change it by asking** takes a change in plain words and shows it before saving, and lists suggestions about the requirements with one-tap fixes. **Candidate view** shows the job as candidates will see it. Admins and ITS recruiters also see **Everything (advanced)**, the whole packet for someone technical. If the editor refuses a save, tell ITS and we make the change with you. A filled or closed role offers **Hire for this role again**, which starts a new draft from it and asks you to confirm the pay again.

Things to check before you approve:

- Is the pay range one you would actually pay, and is the ceiling right?
- Are the must-haves really must-haves? Each one becomes something the assistant looks for evidence of. Six is the maximum; three or four is often better.
- Are the eligibility questions lawful and necessary? They are the only questions that can hold an application.
- Do the interview rounds match how you actually interview, and are the right people named?
- Read the public description as a candidate would.

## 4. Approval

The card **Approve this role packet** lists any assumptions the assistant made and says who can decide. **Preview the job page** opens it exactly as candidates will see it, in a new tab (never public). The card shows what the job page lists of your must-haves and nice-to-haves (**Candidates see this**; why each matters stays with your team), which job boards it goes to, anything the law where the job is still asks of the ad (approving waits for it), and what starts on its own once the job is live. Press **Approve and publish**. The role becomes **Open**, the careers page and job page go live, and a short screen shows where the job is going, each place ticking only once it was checked. While ITS has not yet made its one-time check of your company, the card says so: the role opens here but nothing of it is public, the job page and its Share tab say why the job page shows "not found" for now, and it goes public on its own as soon as ITS confirms your company. Owners, admins and hiring managers can approve, with two rules: you cannot approve a role you started or a version you wrote yourself, unless you are the company's only owner (the card tells you so); and if the packet names a decision maker who is on your team, the approval is theirs, though an owner can also decide. Sending a packet back follows the same rule: the named decision maker, or an owner.

If the card lists wording to check (a phrase that may stand in for a protected trait, or a pay figure in the description that is not the range set for the job), the role cannot be approved until each one is dealt with: press **Change the wording** to edit it, or **Keep it…** and say why. Whoever approves sees who kept it and the reason. Sending the role back is always possible, whatever the card lists.

If something should change first, press **Send back with a note** and say what. The role becomes a draft again and its page shows **Sent back** with your note. Whoever edits it presses **Request approval again** when it is ready; every edit is kept as a version. An approval nobody decides within 14 days goes back to a draft the same way.

Editing after approval creates a new version. If you can approve, your edit takes effect at once; if you can edit but not approve (a recruiter, for instance), the edit waits on the approval card as "Approve packet v3 of ..." while the role keeps running on its current version. If your company narrowed who approves jobs, only those people's edits take effect at once. If you kept wording the fairness check flagged on a live or paused job, a second approver must say yes before it goes public (unless you are the company's only owner), and you cannot approve it yourself; the card says why. When someone else's wording-only edit takes effect at once, the job's named decision maker is told. An edit saved while the job is paused follows the same rules; the toast says it shows when you resume the job, and resuming publishes the newest approved version. A closed job takes no new versions, and the editor tells you so. Candidates who already applied are assessed against the version they applied to.

## 5. The Share tab and the manual posting steps

**Post on LinkedIn.** Right under the public link, this card writes a short announcement for your own LinkedIn feed from the job's facts: the title, the place, the work model, the pay when the job page shows it, up to three must-haves, and the job's LinkedIn link, so applicants from it show as coming from LinkedIn. Pick a tone (**Proud**, **Straight** or **Friendly**) and change the words if you like; a preview and a character count (LinkedIn allows 3,000) sit beside the box. **Copy and open LinkedIn** copies the words and opens LinkedIn in a new tab, where you paste them; **Copy text** only copies. Nothing is posted for you. The card works for a live job once ITS has confirmed your company; until then it says so. On the **Jobs** page, **Post on LinkedIn** on a live job's row opens this card.

One card per place the job appears, each with a status and a sentence saying what happens next. Everyone who works on the role can read this tab. The buttons that change a posting, pause the role or stop applications are for owners and admins (and ITS); if you are a hiring manager and something needs changing, ask your owner or your ITS contact.

- **Careers page**: your job page, served by the product. The address is on the card with **Copy**. The product opens the page and checks it before calling it **Live and verified**.
- **Google for Jobs**: the job page carries the data Google reads. It shows **Submitted** until someone sees the job in Google and marks it verified with a note.
- **Applications by email**: an address for forwarded applications. It works once ITS has connected incoming mail for your workspace; until then the card says **Not connected** and the boards use their own application links.
- **LinkedIn**, **Indeed**, **ZipRecruiter** (the boards ticked for the role): these are posted by ITS by hand under your company's accounts, unless your company chose to post them itself. Here is what happens:
  1. The card shows **Ready to post**, with the steps, **Copy posting package** (everything the board asks for, ready to paste) and the board's own application link. Each board has a different link, so the product knows where each applicant came from.
  2. ITS posts the job on the board with that package and that link. The card says who at ITS has it. If ITS cannot post it, the card says why, and an owner or admin can press **Ask ITS again** or post it from the company's own account.
  3. ITS records the public address of the posting. The card changes to **Posted**.
  4. The product opens the address and checks that it shows your job. If it cannot, it tries twice more, then the card says **Failed** and why; **Check again** repeats it, and a person who can see the posting can press **I checked it myself** and say what they checked.
  5. The card changes to **Live and verified**, with who or what verified it and when. Nothing is called live before this.

When ITS posts, you do not have to do anything on the boards yourself. If a board asks your account holder to confirm something, ITS tells you. After an approved change, a board that still shows the earlier version says so until it is updated. Paid promotion on a board is bought by you, from the board.

**Pausing or closing one board.** **Pause** on a card pauses that posting only, and **Resume** puts it back where it was. **Close** ends it. The job may still show on the board itself until someone takes it down there.

**Still up.** When the role closes, or when one board's posting is closed, the role page shows a red alert naming the boards where the job is still showing. When ITS posts for your company, ITS gets a task to take each one down; when your team posts itself, owners and admins get a card on **Home**. Once the posting is gone from a board, an owner or admin (or ITS) ticks **Removed from this board** on its card. The alert stays until every board is ticked.

**Other links.** Under **Other links** you can make an application link for a referral, a direct approach or anything else. Applications through it carry the name you gave it.

**Applications by source (last 14 days).** How many people applied each day, and through which board or link, in your time zone. If more than 100 people apply in a day, owners and admins are told the next day. Nobody is ever turned away because of the number.

**Pause** in the job page's **More** menu stops new applications without declining anyone: the job page says the position is not accepting applications and the role disappears from your careers page. **Resume** puts it back. If you have enough applicants and want the job to stay listed but closed, use **Stop accepting applications** in the job page's **More** menu instead: the page stays up and says applications are closed, a banner under the title says since when, nobody who applied is affected, and nobody is declined. **Accept applications again** reopens it. Either way, the board postings stay up until they are paused or removed there; tell your ITS contact.

## 6. The Candidates view

**Candidates** shows the candidates for one job (a live job in **Jobs** or on **Home** opens straight onto it). If you have more than one job, pick it at the top right. The line under the title says what the page is for: who applied, what the evidence says, and what needs you. Nobody is declined automatically on judgment.

**The stage strip.** Across the top, each stage with how many people are at it: **All**, **New**, **Longlist**, **Shortlist**, **Interviewing**, **Cancelled**, **On hold**, **Offer**, **Hired** and **Not moving forward**. Press one to see only those people. **Cancelled** holds the candidates who cancelled a booked interview or did not come to one (section 11). Longlist, Shortlist and On hold are private marks your team sets; the candidate never sees them. Beside the strip, **Still applying** shows the people answering the screening conversation in the same visit as their application (they are never counted as waiting on you).

**Narrowing and sorting.** **Waiting on you** keeps only the people a decision waits on, longest wait first; the page opens on it when anyone is waiting. **Not told yet** shows the people whose decision has not been sent to them (section 13). **Match** keeps one overall reading (Strong match, Potential match, Missing a must-have, Needs a closer look), the search box finds a name, email address or source, and **Sort** orders the list by **Score**, **Newest first**, **Waiting longest** or **Name**; both are buttons that name the choice in force and open a short list with it ticked. Every filter that is on shows as a small chip above the list: press one to remove it, or **Clear all**. When the filters leave nobody, the page says **Nothing matches these filters** with one **Clear filters** button. **Switch job** opens the list of your jobs, each with its status, to see another job's candidates. The list is never ordered by the assistant's words.

**The row.** Each candidate shows their initials and name, when they applied, where they came from, how long they have waited on you, a hold's end date, the overall reading, chips such as **Check eligibility**, **Hold ended** or **Not told yet**, the match score, and the stage button. The stage button lists every stage; the ones you cannot choose now are greyed with the reason.

**The overall readings** are **Strong match**, **Potential match**, **Weak match** and **Insufficient evidence**. They are the assistant's words for what the evidence shows against your must-haves and what you look for.

**The match score** sits beside the reading: a number from 0 to 100 the software works out from the checked findings (the assistant never writes it), with the breakdown on the candidate's page: each must-have and skill, how much of it was shown, and how many points it cost, the costliest first. Must-haves count most, then each skill by how important you marked it on the role (Essential, Important or Helpful). Whatever the application does not say is left out, not counted against the person; what it is based on ("Based on 5 of 7 requirements") shows beside the number on the board and on the candidate's page, and when you point at the badge in the list. A red **Missing must-have** chip appears only when the candidate's own words show a must-have is not met, and the score then stays below 55. The colour is green from 70, amber from 55 and plain grey below. The confidence stays a separate chip. A person always decides; sort the list by score whenever it helps.

**Needs a closer look.** When the assistant could not say enough (the reading is Insufficient evidence, or it recommends a person look), the candidate stays in **Waiting on you** with the label **Needs a closer look** and the card says what is missing in plain words: the open questions, or that the resume could not be read. The notification you receive says the same. Two things can be missing that are worth knowing: **The résumé could not be read; the written answers were used** means the file was fine but had no text in it (a photo or a scan), so the assessment rests on what the candidate typed; **We could not assess this application automatically; please review it yourself** means the assistant's answer was unusable twice, so there is no assessment at all. Nothing is hidden in either case: the resume, the answers and the messages are on the candidate's page. Choose **Needs a closer look** under **Reading** to see only those people.

Press the name to open the candidate.

**Comparing candidates.** When a role has more than one candidate, choose **Compare** in the job's **More** menu on Candidates. Tick up to five people and press **Compare selected**. A table puts them side by side: where each person is, then one row per must-have (met, partly met, not met, unknown) and per thing you look for (seen, partly seen, not seen yet, contradicted), then the screen's recommendation, the resume reading, and how much is still unknown. "No evidence yet" means nothing has spoken to that row. The columns are in the order you ticked them, or highest score first if you switch to that, and each person's match score is shown with them. A reading made against an earlier version of the role says so. Press **Done comparing** to go back to the list.

**The pool board and Review and apply.** The board sorts a role's applicants into **Shortlist**, **Longlist** and **Not moving forward** from their evidence, each card with the score and what it rests on, the assistant's confidence, a one-line reason, the must-have line ("Must-haves: 3 of 4 shown, 1 not yet known"), anything already under way ("Invited to the screening conversation automatically"), and a suggested next step (Interview, Short phone call, Ask for more detail, Keep as backup, Read it yourself, Not moving forward). Anyone the board is unsure about goes on the Longlist with **Needs your eye** and the reason: for example when the application says too little about the must-haves to go on, when the screening conversation is still open, or when the assistant's own suggestion and the score disagree. The board never suggests declining someone on a low score built from too little evidence. People still applying, already decided or already sorted by you are shown where they are. **Review and apply** lists the suggestions; untick anyone you want to look at yourself, change the reason for anyone filed as not moving forward, and press **Apply**. Nobody is emailed: it is all private until you send the decision message from the Candidates page, and you are reminded if you have not. **Have a recruiter check this** asks an ITS recruiter to look at the board as it stands.

**Inside the candidate panel.** The candidate opens in a panel beside the list, so the list stays where it was. At the top: their name, when they applied, the match score, **Email**, the stage button, **More** (**Invite to a screen**, **Record an outcome**) and, when you opened them from the list, previous and next (Alt+Up and Alt+Down). If you have typed something you have not sent or saved, moving on asks first.

Under the top, whatever waits on you about this person, each as its own card: something the assistant proposed (such as **The assistant suggests a screen** with **Yes, invite to a screen**, or **Not now**), **How did the interview go?** (section 12), an interview to approve (section 10), an offer waiting on their answer (section 14), **Held on an eligibility answer** or **Declined on an eligibility answer** (section 7), a hold that has ended, a decision not told yet (section 13), and a message a teammate wrote that waits for approval. A stage you choose on the stage button opens its step here too (hold until a date, not moving forward, interview, offer, hire).

Then the sections, as tabs:

- **Overview**: where things stand (a private mark, a hold's date, interviews, whether the decision was told, a recorded outcome), the match score with its breakdown, the latest reading (the screen's scorecard once there is one, otherwise the résumé's; section 9), the eligibility answers, the last three things that happened, **Contact and consent** (email, phone, location, text-message consent, which is off in release 1, and when the AI notice was shown) and **Bring in a recruiter**.
- **Screening chat**: each screening chat with its full transcript, **Stop this screen** while an invitation has not been started, and earlier scorecards.
- **Résumé**: the résumé itself (a Word file shows the words it was read as), with **Full screen** and **Open in a new tab**; each opening is recorded. Below it, **What the résumé says**: the positions with dates and a quoted line from the résumé for each, licenses, skills, and any flags. A file that is still being checked says so; a refused file says why.
- **Messages**: everything sent to the candidate and whether it arrived, anything they wrote back (including email replies once ITS has connected incoming mail), and **Write to (first name)** (section 15).
- **Notes**: your hiring team's private notes on the candidate (section 12).
- **History**: the full timeline with who did what, including what the assistant suggested and who approved it.

A small dot on a tab means something new arrived there since you last opened it.

**What the assistant recommends, and what it never does.** The assistant recommends: it may say a candidate is worth a screen, worth an interview, worth a hold, or that it has a concern. It never declines anyone. It never writes the match score: the software works it out from the checked findings and shows how. It never sends a decline message. If it cannot produce an assessment, you see the application without one. If an assessment looks wrong, use **Something wrong?** on it (section 9): an ITS recruiter is asked to check it, and you can follow the request on Home.

## 7. Held on an eligibility answer

When a candidate answers no to a required eligibility question, the card reads **Held on an eligibility answer** with the question and the answer. Nothing is sent to the candidate. You are reminded daily, and an ITS recruiter is brought in after three days if nobody decides.

- If the answer was a mistake or you want to keep them anyway, type why under **Why keep them in?** and press **Keep them in**. Your reason is recorded.
- If you agree they do not meet the requirement, press **Decline now**, read what follows, and press **Confirm decline**. Nothing happens on the first press. The decision is private: nothing is sent to the candidate until someone sends the decision message (section 13), which then explains it was their eligibility answer.

A candidate declined this way shows **Declined on an eligibility answer** with a **Review anyway** button in case you change your mind.

## 8. The screening conversation

**In the same visit as applying (pilot roles that ITS has switched on).** Everyone who passes your eligibility questions goes straight from the application form to a short written conversation with your AI assistant. Nobody has to invite them. While they are in it they show apart, under **Still applying**, and you are not alerted about them until they finish, send their application without it, or stop part-way. You do not see their answers until they press Send: until then their page says "Still applying: their answers appear here once they send them".

What the candidate sees, on a phone or a computer, with no app and no password:

- First, a short introduction: it is your company's AI assistant, a software program, not a person; it is a conversation, not a test; about how many questions and minutes (five or six questions, about ten minutes, is the usual length); they can skip a question or stop and come back. Three buttons: **Start**, **Do this later** (the link goes to them by email) and **I'd rather talk to a person**. Every page of the conversation says at the top that it is the AI assistant.
- Then one question at a time, in your packet's own words, with "Question 2 of 5" and a thin progress bar. They answer in their own words; for simple facts (yes or no, which days, a start date, the pay they are looking for) they can tap an answer instead. The assistant may ask a short follow-up that quotes what they said. **Skip this question** is always there; a skipped question counts as unknown, never against them.
- At the end, **Check your answers**: every answer with **Change**, then **Send my answers**. You are alerted when they press Send, and only then do their answers, the scorecard and anything worked out from them appear: on their page, in **Compare**, on the board, in the one-click questions, in the match score, on **Home**, in the role report and its files, and in an interviewer's brief. Nothing sends their answers for them. Before that you can open them from **Still applying**, but the **Screen** tab shows only "Still applying: their answers appear here once they send them", and the **Messages** tab leaves out the conversation's own messages and the reminders about it. The candidate is told that the hiring team sees their answers only once they send them.
- A conversation that runs long is brought to a close by the software: the candidate goes to **Check your answers**, where they can still change any answer and answer any question left open. No closing line promises them a time to hear back; it says people decide and they will hear either way.

**I'd rather talk to a person** gives the candidate two equal choices: **Send without the conversation** (their application comes to you as it is) or **Have a person contact me** (an ITS recruiter gets in touch within four business hours; ITS covers it, and it does not use your plan's recruiter requests). Both are counted on the role's figures. Anything they answered before choosing was never sent, so neither you nor the ITS recruiter sees it; the recruiter goes through the questions on a call and adds notes from it, which you see on the **Screen** tab, labelled as the recruiter's notes and not the candidate's own words. If the assistant itself hands the conversation to a person, the request says only that: never how the assistant thought they sounded.

If they stop part-way, they get one reminder the next day; two days after that the conversation closes as unfinished and they are told nothing else is needed. If they answered everything but did not press Send, the reminder asks them to check and send, tells them the hiring team sees their answers only once they send them, and gives the day the conversation closes if they do not; two days later it closes the same way. Either way they never sent their answers, so you never see them and nothing is read from them: their page says "Didn't finish the conversation", in the same words however far they got, and their reading and match score come from their application alone. The role's figures count how many didn't finish, never the question anyone stopped at. If they never start it, they get one reminder after two days; three days after that their application comes to you without it, and they are told that is fine. If the assistant stops working, the conversation pauses with their answers kept and they are emailed a link to finish once it works again; after a day without it, a person takes over and they are told so. A person is always one click away.

Each conversation counts against your plan's monthly allowance at the candidate's first answer, never at the invitation. At 80% you and ITS are warned. If the allowance runs out, applications keep coming in, and people who apply meanwhile get the link by email once more conversations are added or the allowance starts again (every 30 days; **Settings**, **Company**, **Usage** says when), so everyone for a role has the same process.

**Later, by email (every role ITS has not switched to the same visit, and any role whose screen invitations are "Ask me first" or "Only a person").** Candidates send the form only. Under the default rule the assistant invites candidates whose applications look like a fit and tells you under **Recent activity**; you can also invite anyone yourself: open the candidate and press **Invite to a screen**. With "Ask me first" you see **The assistant suggests a screen** and press **Yes, invite to a screen**. The candidate gets an email with the link; the page opens on the same introduction, and the conversation works as above. If they have not started after two days they get one reminder. If they have not started three days after that, the invitation lapses, the candidate shows "No response", and you are told. You can then decline with a reason, keep them, or ask a recruiter to call.

**What the candidate sees after applying.** Their status page opens with a progress line: **Applied**, **Screening chat** (only when they have or had one), **Review**, **Interview** (at the decision, only if times were offered or an interview was booked) and **Decision**. Steps passed are ticked and the current one is marked; under it, **What happens next** says the next thing in one line, with the one button they need. A private decision changes nothing on that line until they are told. Messages, withdrawing, privacy requests and every link stay below it. Dates on the page are in your company's time zone. **Shared this link by mistake?** lets them press **Get a new link**: the old link (and their screening chat and interview links) stops working at once, and the new one is emailed only to the address on their application, never shown on the page. Withdrawing in the screening chat is recorded and confirmed by email, the same as from the status page.

**Applications that arrive by email** are not recorded as having accepted the application terms and the AI notice they never saw. The assistant reads them only after our acknowledgment email, which carries the AI notice line and their status link, has gone; if it never goes, the assistant does not read it and you review it yourself.

**What the assistant sees when it scores.** When it reads a résumé and when it writes the screening scorecard, the assistant sees placeholders such as [name], [email] and [phone] instead of who the candidate is, and nothing about their earlier applications to your company. Quotes you see on a reading are still the candidate's own words.

## 9. Reading a scorecard

When the screen is complete, the candidate's page gets a **Screen scorecard** and the candidate moves to **Waiting on you**. It has:

- three chips: the overall reading, the recommendation (**Worth a screen**, **Hold**, **Needs a closer look**, **Strong: interview**, **Interview** or **Concern raised**), and the confidence
- a summary, and under it why the confidence is what it is ("Confidence is low because no résumé, only the written summary; two must-haves have no evidence")
- **Pay expectation** and **Availability** if the candidate stated them, with whether they fit your range and schedule
- **Must-haves**: each one with yes, partially, no or unknown, and the quoted evidence
- **Answers**: what each screening answer showed
- **Competencies**: what was evidenced, partially evidenced, not evidenced or contradicted
- **Strengths** and **Concerns**, each with its source and a quote
- **Unknown**: what could not be established and why it matters
- flags, which are never used to decline, and the assistant's reasoning

The **Resume assessment**, written when the application arrived, has the same shape without the answers.

How the readings are kept steady and honest:

- Both the résumé reading and the scorecard are written three times and the middle answer is kept, so the same résumé gets the same reading.
- **Licences and certificates**: a licence, certificate, permit or registration must-have reads **no** only when the candidate says plainly they do not hold it. Lapsed, expired, pending, applied for, in training or unclear reads **unknown**, and the screening chat can ask about it.
- **Quotes**: only the candidate's own words count as evidence, never words the assistant recorded or rephrased, and a bare "yes" or a single word is not evidence. A **yes** with no quote counts as partly met in the match score, and the breakdown shows **no quote**.
- A skill you set to be judged **in the interviews** is not read from résumés or chats and does not move the score.
- When you keep someone in after an eligibility answer (**Keep them in** or **Review anyway**), the reading is told and treats them as eligible. Inviting them to a screening chat stays your call.
- Every question or follow-up the assistant writes for a screening chat is checked before the candidate sees it; one that touches age, family, religion, origin, health, past pay or similar topics is never sent, and the chat moves to the next question.

Read the evidence, not just the chips. Unknowns are questions to ask; under them, **Ask about this in a screen** invites the candidate to a short web screen that starts with exactly those questions before the usual ones (the invitation says so: "a few quick questions about your background"). The answers appear on the candidate's page beside the questions once they finish the conversation. When a screen is already open the button gives way to a sentence saying so, since the invitation cannot be sent twice. If something is wrong, use **Something wrong?** on the card and pick the reason: **Cited the wrong evidence**, **Made something up**, **Broke a rule we set**, **Bias concern**, **Poorly worded**, **Took the wrong next step**, **Missed a good candidate**, **Overrated this candidate** or **Missed context we gave**. A person at ITS reviews every flag, which is attached to all three runs behind the reading. Flagging changes nothing about the candidate.

## 10. Approving an interview and picking interviewers

Nothing schedules until a person approves it. Open the candidate and choose **Interviewing** on the stage button. If the assistant proposed it, you see **The assistant suggests an interview**; **Yes, interview** (there or on Home) opens the approval below already filled in from your interview plan, so it is one more look and one click, never a second approval. Someone already in Interviewing with nothing scheduled (you cancelled an interview, they did not pick a time, you chose the next round, or you pressed **Not now**) has a card at the top of their page with **Approve an interview** (or **Approve the next round**), which opens the same approval. Someone under **Cancelled** has their own card instead, with **Send new times** (section 11). When a candidate does not pick a time, the interview's own page also offers **Offer new times** for the same round and interviewers.

Under **Approve an interview**:

1. **Round**: the next round from the interview plan is selected. Change it if needed. A round must be in the approved plan.
2. **Interviewers**: the round's interviewers from the plan who are still on your team are ticked. Each shows **availability known** or **no calendar or hours yet**. Someone with no availability cannot be offered times; they will be asked to set it in Settings, and scheduling waits for them.
3. **Meeting link**: **Create it with the calendar event**, or **I'll paste a link** and paste your own video or phone details.
4. **A personal line for the invitation** (optional): one or two sentences that go with the times, under your name. **Suggest a line** asks the assistant for one; change it or leave it out, and if you keep it the invitation says the assistant helped, even after you change a few words (empty the line, or press **Take out the assistant's line**, to leave that out). It is checked like the personal line on a decision message (section 13).
5. Press **Approve and offer times**.

Times are worked out from each interviewer's connected calendar or declared hours, within a scheduling window, on business days (public holidays are never offered), respecting the buffer and the daily maximum (counted by each interviewer's own day). Nobody is assumed free: until every interviewer has a calendar or hours, no times are offered, you are told who is missing, and only they are asked to add their hours. If the interviewers have no common free time, you are told and can change interviewers or free some time. As soon as an interviewer saves new hours, every interview waiting on them looks again at once. Whenever new times are offered (after a reschedule, a "none of these work", or late availability), they come from a fresh window that starts tomorrow and is as long as the first one, never from days that have passed.

## 11. The candidate picking a time

The candidate gets an email with a link to "Pick a time for your (round)". They see the times in their own time zone (they can change it), pick one, can add a note under "Anything that would make the interview easier for you?", and press **Confirm this time**. If none work they press **Ask for other times** with a note; you are told, and other times are emailed to them as soon as there are some that differ from the ones already offered.

When they confirm, the product books the time on every connected calendar, sends the candidate a confirmation with a calendar file, tells the interviewers by email and on Home, and moves the candidate to "Interviewing". A pick is never lost: if the times had already moved on, the time is booked straight away when it is still free, and if it was just booked for another interview the candidate is asked to choose another. If a chosen time was taken in the meantime, new times go out on their own; after three real collisions an ITS recruiter arranges it directly.

The candidate gets one calendar invitation per interview, ours, with the meeting link; connected interviewers' calendars do not send them a second one.

The candidate can **Ask for a different time** twice on their own and can **Cancel the interview**, until the interview starts; after that their page says it can no longer be moved there. Either way you are told.

**When a candidate cancels.** To cancel, the candidate chooses what they would like next: **Send me new times later** or **I'm no longer interested**. They move to **Cancelled** on the job's Candidates page, with their choice beside their name ("Asked for new times" or "No longer interested"), and you are told which they chose. A candidate you record as **Candidate did not show** (section 12) goes there too, as "No-show".

- **Automatic new times.** Someone who asked for new times gets one set by email automatically, for the same interview with the same interviewers, after the working days your company chose in Settings under **When a candidate cancels** (3 unless changed; it can be switched off). The candidate is told the day. Their row says "New times go out automatically" with that day. It is one set only: if they do not pick a time, they stay under Cancelled for you to decide.
- **Nothing goes automatically** to someone who said they are no longer interested, someone who did not come, someone who had already moved the interview twice, someone you had put on hold, anyone while an ITS recruiter is running the role, or anyone before ITS has confirmed your company. A reply of HUMAN or "no thanks" stops times that were due. Their card says why, and they wait for you.
- **Send new times** on their card (or on the interview's own page) sends times now, for the same round: the interview they cancelled is offered again, or, after a no-show, a new interview for that round is set up. On their card it asks first; on the interview's own page the button sends at once. The toast says "Getting new times ready" (after a no-show, that a new interview for the same round was set up): the times go out as soon as there are times to offer from the interviewers' calendars. **Don't send automatically** stops times that were due to go by themselves; they stay under Cancelled. If nobody who ran the interview is still on your team, **Choose interviewers** opens the interview approval.
- **Everyone at once.** Under Cancelled, the chips **Asked for new times**, **No longer interested** and **No-show** show each group, and **Send new times…** sends to several people at once. People who said they are no longer interested start unticked, and anyone whose new times are already being prepared or are out cannot be ticked. Each person's own result is listed.
- **Leaving Cancelled.** They leave it when they book a time, when you move them (on hold, a decision, the next round), when you cancel the interview carrying their new times, or when they leave the process. Sending new times does not move them; booking does.

If the candidate does not pick a time: a reminder after two days, and after four days the times expire and you are told to offer new times, decide, or bring in a recruiter.

Reminders go to the candidate and the interviewers one day before and two hours before.

**On the Interviews page** you see every interview **In motion** (Preparing times, Waiting on the candidate, Scheduled) and **Past**, with the interviewer load. Open one to see details, the times offered, an accommodation request if any, and to change it:

- **Book this for them**, next to an offered time, if you agreed a time by phone. It is recorded as a booking made by you on their behalf. If that time was just taken by another interview, you are told and nothing is booked.
- **Offer new times** to reschedule from your side. If a time was already booked, the candidate is told at once in one email that it is off (with a calendar file that removes it, and your reason if you gave one), then gets the new times; the other interviewers are told on Home.
- **Cancel interview** with a reason the candidate sees, then **Confirm cancel**. The candidate and interviewers are told.
- **Message the candidate** opens the candidate's page with the message box ready (section 15).
- After the interview, **After the interview** shows the decision, or **Record the decision**, which takes you to the candidate's page (section 12).

Interviewers receive the booking, the reminders and the link, and a brief: in the confirmation email and on the interview page under **Your brief**. It says what the round is for, what to look for, the questions the plan suggests, how the candidate stands on your must-haves, strengths so far, what is worth exploring, and what they said in the screen. Interviewers never see pay. They can open only the candidates they interview, and they do not see the Talent list.

## 12. Recording the decision after an interview

After the interview, the interviewers (and anyone who manages interviews) see **Did it take place?** on the interview page: **Yes**, **Candidate did not show**, or **We did not show**. Answer it; it keeps the record straight, and it can be answered once. A no-show is never held against anyone automatically. **We did not show** means our interviewer missed it: the candidate's card then offers **Approve the same round again** first, the approval proposes that round, and the new invitation opens with an apology; you can still pick another round or decide. **Candidate did not show** puts the candidate under **Cancelled** as a no-show: nothing goes to them automatically, and you send new times for the same round or decide (their decision card then offers no **Next round**).

Once the interview is over, the candidate waits on you: they appear under **Waiting on you** in Candidates, the people who decide get a notification ("How did it go with ...?") that opens the candidate's page, and **Home** shows a card. On the candidate's page you see **How did the interview go?**. The decision is yours and is recorded with your name; nothing here is scored by software.

- **Next round**: the candidate moves on. The page opens the interview approval for the next round with its interviewers; check them and approve, and times go out from there.
- **Finalist**: write what stood out under **What stood out (kept internally)** and press **Confirm finalist**.
- **Not moving forward**: pick a reason, add a note if you like, and press **Confirm: not moving forward**. The decision is private: nothing reaches the candidate until someone sends the decision message (section 13).
- **Hired**: opens the hire sheet (section 14), which asks for the start date.

Release 1 has no interviewer scorecards. Record what matters in the note. The decision shows on the interview page too.

**Team notes.** Each person on the hiring team keeps one private note on a candidate, under **Notes** on their page and on the **How did the interview go?** card. Start from a prompt (**Before the interview**, **After the interview**, **For and against**) or write freely. It saves when you stop typing; changes made in one sitting count as one version, and your own earlier versions are listed under your note. Only you can change your note. Owners, admins, hiring managers, HR and an ITS recruiter you brought in read every note, also side by side in **Compare**; an interviewer writes and reads only their own note, on someone they interviewed, once the interview has started. Notes are private to your hiring team: the candidate never sees them, and they are never given to the AI assistant. Stick to job-related facts, and write nothing you would not want the candidate to read.

Owners, admins and hiring managers record these decisions (and an ITS recruiter you brought in). HR and interviewers can say whether an interview took place but cannot decide.

## 13. Declining with a reason

At any stage, open the candidate, press the stage button and choose **Not moving forward**. (Someone held on an eligibility answer is declined from that answer's own card instead, section 7.) A caution reminds you that declining is your decision and is recorded with your reason. Pick a **Reason**:

- **Experience isn't the right fit**
- **Missing skills we need**
- **Pay expectations don't match**
- **Location or schedule**
- **Position filled**
- **Other**

Add a **Note (kept internally)** if you like and press **Confirm: not moving forward**. The decision is private: it is not sent to the candidate, and their status page still shows the step they were at. If they had an interview booked or times offered, it is cancelled now, and they get a short note straight away saying only that the interview is off (no reason given), so nobody turns up to an interview that no longer exists. The confirmation names that interview, with its day and time, before you press the button. The decision message you send later does not mention the interview again. Declined candidates appear under **Not moving forward** with **Not told yet**, and the reason is on their timeline. A candidate is never declined by software.

**Changed your mind before telling them?** While the candidate has not been told, no message to them waits for approval and the role is still open, **Take back this decision** on their **Not told yet** card (it asks you to confirm), or the earlier stage on the stage button, puts them back where they were. Nothing was sent and nothing will be; the history records that you took it back. A screen or an interview the decision called off is not reopened, so approve a new one if you want it. Once they have been told, a decision cannot be taken back this way. Someone declined on an eligibility answer has **Review anyway** instead (section 7).

**Telling the candidate.** Telling is a separate step, by a person. On the candidate's page, **Send message** shows the exact message first. You can pick one of a few approved versions of the wording (the screen suggests the one that fits, for example "After an interview" or "Position filled"); the message never gives your reason. You can also add a short **personal line** of your own, one or two sentences. It is checked before anything is sent: it can't mention scores or ratings, what the assistant found, the reason you recorded, or anything personal such as age, family, health, religion or background, and if it does you are told exactly what to take out. The bottom of every message stays the same and can't be edited: whether we keep their details for future roles and how to say no, how to reach a person, and the AI notice.

**Send** sends it (under **Ask me first** it waits for a second person to approve it, with your wording and line; under **Only a person** the product sends no decision messages). For 30 seconds after you press Send you can press **Undo**: the message is taken back and they stay not told. To tell several people at once, use **Not told yet** on the Candidates page, then **Review and send**: each person gets their own message, in the wording that fits their decision unless you pick one for everyone, you see what happened for each, and **Undo all** takes back every message still waiting. If you told them yourself (by phone, say), press **I told them myself** and nothing more is sent. Only once they were told does their status page show the decision.

**If nobody tells them.** Everyone not told yet is listed on **Home**, and after two working days you get a reminder. After five working days (Monday to Friday, holidays left out), the standard message goes to them by itself and you are told who got it. If your settings ask for approval of decision messages, keep them to a person, or an ITS recruiter is running the role, nothing is sent: an ITS recruiter is brought in to make sure they hear from you. The candidate's page names the day this happens. Someone whose details were deleted at their request is never written to.

**Candidates in Colorado** get one more line at the bottom of the message saying they can ask for the main reasons for the decision and for a person to review it. If they reply asking, the request goes to the ITS privacy and compliance team, who answer them. You are told that they asked, and their reply is on their page like any reply to your email; ITS handles the answer. (The wording is a draft our lawyer is reviewing.)

**On hold** keeps a candidate where they are while you think, until a date you choose (two weeks unless you change it); they come back to **Home** then. It sends nothing.

## 14. Recording the outcome

The product never makes or sends an offer, and it asks for no pay, tax, identity or banking details. You make offers your own way and record what happened.

**Offer made.** When you have made a finalist (or someone whose references you are checking) an offer, open them and press **Offer made**, or choose **Offer** on the stage button. Pick the day it was made (today unless you change it), add a private note if you like, and press **Record the offer**. Nothing is sent to them, and no term of the offer is kept. They move to **Offer**, under **Waiting on the candidate**, and if no answer is recorded after five days you are reminded to check in with them. When they answer, record it on their page: **They accepted** opens the hire sheet; **They declined** (or **They withdrew**) records that. Owners, admins, hiring managers and HR record offers and outcomes.

**The hire sheet.** **Hire**, **They accepted**, **Hired** after an interview, or **Hired** on the stage button all open it. Enter the start date (and the day they accepted, if you know it), press **Continue**, then **Yes, record the hire**: it is recorded under your name, and nothing is sent to anyone. The sheet then lists the other people still in the running for the role and offers two choices:

- **Close it now** (the role, as filled): everyone still in process moves to your talent pool, privately, unless they asked not to be kept; anyone with an interview booked or times offered gets a short note straight away saying only that the interview is off; and you see the closing message before it goes (below).
- **Keep the role open**: nobody is told anything, and **Home** shows a "Keep your backups warm" card until the start date, in case the start falls through. If the start date you entered has already passed, there is no card to show, so the sheet offers **Leave it open** instead and says so: close the role from its page when you are ready.

**Record an outcome** (in the candidate page's menu) opens **Record what happened** for the other outcomes. **Open the hire sheet** is at the top; otherwise choose:

- **They declined the offer made outside the platform**
- **The candidate withdrew**
- **We declined the candidate**: pick a reason from the same list as **Not moving forward** (section 13), and add a note if you like. Like any decline it is private until someone sends the decision message (section 13). Shown only to people who may decline candidates.
- **No hire for this candidate**: they stay in your talent pool unless they asked not to be kept; their page then offers **Send message**, with the message you can send them when you are ready.

The card says before you confirm whether anything goes to the candidate: nothing about the outcome ever does, but an interview they had booked or times they were offered are called off with a short note saying only that it is off. Press **Continue**, then **Yes, record it**. The record carries your name and can only be corrected by an ITS administrator, with the original kept. Closing a role is always a separate decision, from the hire sheet or the role page.

**The closing message.** When you close a role, the close card says how many people were still in process and shows one courteous closing message for all of them (emailed applications that were waiting are included). Press **Send** once to tell everyone: each person gets their own copy, and nobody is told twice (anyone you already told is left out). For 30 seconds, **Undo all** takes every one of them back. Under **Ask me first**, one approval covers the whole group. They stay in your talent pool unless they click "Don't keep me for future roles" on their status page or reply "no thanks" to a decision message. Only people who may decline candidates send the closing message: HR can close a role, and is then told who sends it; until someone does, the people it left wait as **Not told yet**, and the safety net (section 13) applies to them too.

**While they wait.** When people have waited on you for days, **Home** names whoever has waited longest, with **Decide**, **Hold until a date** and **Send a note** (in your own words); nothing is sent from the card. Nobody gets an automatic "still reviewing" note unless your company switches it on: with many applicants you pick the best few and keep in touch yourself. To have it sent, choose **Do it** for **Send a "still reviewing" note** in Settings; then someone still waiting for a decision 30 days after applying gets one short note saying their application is still being reviewed and that a person decides. The note goes only in the week after the 30 days are reached, so turning it on never writes to people who applied months ago. A candidate whose screening link closed without being started is told so, and that they can reply for a new one.

## 15. Writing to a candidate

On the candidate's page, press **Email** at the top (or open the **Messages** tab): the **Write to (first name)** card is under the messages so far. (From an interview, **Message the candidate** opens it for you.) Add a subject if you like, write your message, and press **Send**.

- It goes out under your company's hiring-team name, with your name at the bottom. It is your own words, so it carries no AI notice. The card shows who it goes to and the footer before you send.
- **Suggest an opening line** asks the assistant for one friendly sentence for an update (the interview approval has **Suggest a line** for the invitation, section 10). Change it or leave it out; if you keep it, the email says the assistant helped you write it, even after you change a few words (empty the box, or press **Take out the assistant's line**, to leave that out). The assistant never writes a decline.
- For 30 seconds after you press **Send** you can press **Undo** and nothing goes.
- Under the default settings it goes at once and the owners are told. If your owner has set **Send a message a team member wrote to a candidate** to Ask me first or Only a person, the button says **Send for approval**: an owner or admin reads it first, on **Home** or on the candidate's page, and presses **Yes, send it** or **Do not send**. You are told either way. Messages from owners and admins always go at once.
- A candidate with no email address on file cannot be written to; the card says so.
- Replies: once ITS has connected incoming mail for your workspace, the candidate's reply appears under **Messages** on their page and you are told. Until then, replies reach an ITS mailbox that a person reads. The card tells you which.
- The message itself is on the candidate's page. The audit history records who wrote it and who approved it, never the text.

## 16. The report

The **Report** tab on the role page shows the role's figures, each defined at the bottom of the tab:

- **Who applied**: by source (careers page, each board's link, referral links, email) and by day.
- **Where everyone is**: how many people are at each step, grouped as waiting on you, in process, not moving forward and hired. Cancelled counts the candidates who cancelled an interview or did not come to one; the applicant download has a **Cancelled** column with each one's reason.
- **How long each step takes**: the typical and average days, and the longest anyone has waited.
- **Interviews**: requested, on the calendar, held, missed, cancelled, rescheduled by the candidate, and waiting for your decision.
- **Decisions waiting for you**, **What the assistant suggested, and what you decided**, **When you overruled the system**.
- **Recruiter help**, **Messages to candidates** (including any that could not be delivered, so you can reach those people another way), and **Outcomes**.

**Download the applicant list (CSV)** and **Download the summary (CSV)** give you spreadsheets. Every download is recorded. Interviewers cannot open the report.

## 17. When to bring in a recruiter

Press **Bring in a recruiter** whenever you want a human opinion. Good moments:

- You have several plausible candidates and want a recruiter to review them before you decide.
- You are unsure whether to interview someone.
- You do not have time to run the role and would like ITS to run the hire for you.
- A candidate cannot be reached, or something about a candidate needs a person.

Choose **Review these candidates**, **Help me decide** or **Ask ITS to run this hire**, add anything specific, and press **Send to a recruiter**. Asking ITS to run the hire starts a conversation: an ITS recruiter contacts you to agree how they would run it, and nothing changes until you have both agreed. Before you send, the form tells you what the recruiter will see (one candidate and their role, one role and its candidates, or all of your hiring), the time they will reply by, and which of your plan's recruiter requests this one would be. **Urgent** puts the request at the front of the ITS queue; the reply-by time stays the same.

The recruiter sees what the request covers: the role, its candidates and their messages and decisions so far, or one candidate. Their access starts when they pick the request up, ends a day after it is answered or after 14 days at most, and every page they open is recorded; an owner or admin can end it at any time in **Settings**. You are told when a recruiter picks your request up.

Your requests are listed on **Home** under **Your recruiter requests**, with their state, the reply-by time and who picked each one up. The recruiter's answer appears there in full, and you are told when it comes in; it stays on Home for two weeks, and older requests fold under **Older requests**. While a request is open you can press **Add a note for the recruiter**. Once it is answered, if the answer did not solve it, press **This didn't solve it** and say what is still missing: the request opens again with a new reply-by time. It goes back to the recruiter who answered when they can still take it, otherwise to the ITS queue, and whoever has it gets fresh access, which your team is told about and which is recorded like any other.
