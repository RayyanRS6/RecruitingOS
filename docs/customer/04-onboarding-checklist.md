# Onboarding checklist

**ITS AI Recruiting Department, Founding Partner Program. Release 1.**

Draft of 30 September 2026, for review by ITS before it is given to a customer. This is the order of events from signing to your first candidate, who does each step, and what you need to have ready.

## Who does what

- **ITS**: your ITS contact and the ITS recruiters.
- **Owner**: the person at your company who signs the agreement and owns the workspace. Often the business owner or office manager.
- **Hiring manager**: the person who runs the hire, describes the role and decides on candidates. This can be the same person as the owner.
- **Interviewers**: the people who will meet candidates.

## What to have ready before you start

- **Your sign-in accounts.** A Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 work account for each person who will use the product, with two-step verification switched on. A Microsoft 365 company is the simplest fit.
- **Your job-board accounts.** An employer account on LinkedIn (a company page), Indeed and ZipRecruiter that your company owns or is authorized to use. ITS posts under these accounts. Decide who at your company administers each one.
- **Your company basics.** Company name, what kind of business, roughly how many employees, city and state, website.
- **A short description of your company** for the job page: two or three sentences a candidate would want to read.
- **The role.** The job title, where the job is, whether it is on site, the schedule, the pay range and the most you would pay, why you are hiring, the target start date, the must-haves (up to six), and what you would like but do not require.
- **Who interviews and who decides.** Names and work email addresses.
- **Who handles privacy questions on your side**, if a candidate writes to you directly.

## The steps

| # | Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sign the agreement | Owner, ITS | The agreement with its exhibits (the feature sheet, the security overview, the support statement) is signed. You also receive this checklist and the two guides. ITS issues the first invoice under the agreement. No payment processor is involved; you pay the invoice the way the agreement says |
| 2 | Kick-off call | Owner, hiring manager, ITS | About forty-five minutes. ITS confirms your ITS contact, the support and privacy addresses, the weekly meeting time, and which of your people get which role. ITS walks through the feature sheet so nobody expects something the product does not do |
| 3 | Sign in and create the workspace | Owner | Go to the sign-in page and press **Continue with Google** or **Continue with Microsoft**. Fill in **Tell us about your company** and press **Create my hiring workspace**. The person who creates the workspace is its owner |
| 4 | Invite the team | Owner | In **Setup**, under **Team**, enter each person's work email, choose their role (admin, hiring manager, interviewer, hr) and press **Invite**. Each person gets an email. The link works once and for seven days |
| 5 | Everyone signs in once | Everyone invited | Open the invitation email, follow the link and sign in with Google or Microsoft using the same work address. Check that the workspace name shows at the top left |
| 6 | Interviewers set their availability | Interviewers, hiring manager | In **Setup**, under **My availability for interviews**, each interviewer sets their time zone, the days and hours they can interview, the buffer between interviews and the most interviews per day, then presses **Save availability**. If ITS has enabled Google Calendar for your workspace, they can press **Connect Google Calendar** instead. Until someone has availability, interview times cannot be offered |
| 7 | Review what the assistant may do on its own | Owner | In **Setup**, under **What the AI may do on its own**, read the list. The defaults are the recommended settings. The decisions that always stay with a person are marked "always a person" and cannot be changed |
| 8 | Give ITS access to your job-board accounts | Owner, ITS | Add the ITS recruiter named by your ITS contact as a user on your LinkedIn company page, your Indeed employer account and your ZipRecruiter account, or agree another way with ITS. Do not send passwords by email. ITS confirms it can sign in to each |
| 9 | The intake conversation | Hiring manager | On **Today**, in **Your recruiting chief of staff**, describe the role in a sentence or two. Answer the assistant's questions. When it says "I have enough to draft the role packet", press **Draft the role packet** |
| 10 | Review the role packet | Hiring manager, owner, ITS | On the role page, read every tab: Overview, Requirements, How we'll evaluate, Interview plan, Posting. Check the pay range and the ceiling, the work model, the must-haves (each says why it matters and where it is checked), the eligibility questions, the screening questions and the interview plan. Under **What matters to you**, check the deal-breakers and who makes the final decision. Press **Edit first** to change anything. ITS reviews the packet with you in the kick-off week if you want a second pair of eyes |
| 11 | Approve the role | Owner or hiring manager | Press **Approve and post**. The person who ran the intake cannot approve their own role unless they are the company's only owner, so where you have two people the other one approves; if the packet names a decision maker on your team, the approval is theirs (an owner can also decide). To ask for changes instead, press **Send back with a note**: the role becomes a draft again with the note on its page, and **Request approval again** sends it back once it is edited. Approval opens the role: the careers page and job page go live, the Google job data is on the page, and the Posting tab shows what ITS has to do by hand |
| 12 | ITS posts to the boards | ITS | ITS posts the job to LinkedIn, Indeed and ZipRecruiter under your accounts, each with its own application link. ITS records each posting's public address; the product checks it; the posting is then marked live. Until then it shows as not yet live |
| 13 | First applications arrive | Nobody has to do anything | Each candidate gets a confirmation and a status page. The resume is checked and scanned, the assistant reads it, eligibility answers are checked, and the assessment appears in **Talent**. Under the default settings the assistant invites candidates who look like a fit to a screening conversation and tells you |
| 14 | First decisions | Hiring manager | Open **Today** each morning. Review candidates under **Waiting on you** in **Talent**. Invite to a screen, hold, interview, or decline with a reason. Press **Bring in a recruiter** whenever you want a second opinion |

## Week one

- **Every business day**, your ITS contact checks your workspace: applications arriving, messages delivered, postings live, anything waiting on you for more than a day. You get a short note if something needs attention.
- **The hiring manager** opens Today each morning and clears what needs them. Ten minutes is usually enough.
- **The owner** checks Setup once to confirm the team and the interviewers' availability are right.
- **By the end of the week** you should have: the job live on your careers page and on the three boards, applications with their source showing, the first screening conversations completed, and the first interview approved and scheduled.
- **The first weekly meeting** at the end of the week: what worked, what confused anyone, what to change in the packet, and the first written summary from ITS.

## After week one

- The weekly meeting and the written summary continue for the program.
- When you have hired, record the outcome on the candidate and close the role. The product lists the boards where the posting is still up; ITS takes them down and confirms.
- Candidates who were still active stay in your talent pool for the next role.
