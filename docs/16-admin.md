## The ITS check of a company and job-board access

Founder decision B7 (plan B1 and B2). A company that signs itself up can draft and approve roles at once, but nothing of it is public (careers page, job pages and their addresses, the JobPosting data, the feeds and sitemap, tracked links, the apply form) and its messages to candidates wait in the outbox as `held` until an administrator confirms it. The ITS queue mailbox is emailed when such a company signs up. Companies that existed before the check began were confirmed by migration 0027. Under `SIGNUP_MODE=approval` this holds for a platform administrator and an address on `SIGNUP_ALLOWED_EMAILS`; anyone else can do nothing until accepted, and Accept is this check ("Asking for access", next).

- **Confirm the company** (`POST /api/v1/admin/customers/:id/verification`, `verified: true`): after checking the company is real (the kick-off call, its website, a phone number). A reason of five characters or more and a second press are required. Its pages go public, approved roles waiting to be published are published, and every held message goes into the queue, oldest first (texts wait for the company's allowed hours); a held message that must no longer go (the candidate stopped texts, or an invitation to someone who has since left the process) is cancelled with its reason instead. Confirming a company that is already confirmed changes nothing except that any message still held goes out: use it to repair a message held while the confirmation was being made. A company asking for access, or declined, answers 409 `access_request_open`: its one door is Accept.
- **Take back the confirmation** (`verified: false`): the pages and feeds go offline at once and messages to candidates wait again, including ones already queued. Roles, candidates and decisions are kept. Today tells the company "ITS is checking your company again".
- Both are audited with the reason (the customer sees it in their own history) and the owners, admins and hiring managers are told; that notice is always emailed and cannot be turned off.
- **Job-board access** (`POST /api/v1/admin/customers/:id/job-boards`): record once ITS can sign in to the company's LinkedIn, Indeed and ZipRecruiter accounts (or that it no longer can, with an optional note). It ticks the owner's getting-started step and is audited.

## Asking for access

First sign-in asks for approval (the owner's request of 6 November 2026; `docs/workflow-decisions.md`, "6 November 2026"). It needs `SIGNUP_MODE=approval`. Someone signed in who is neither a platform administrator nor on `SIGNUP_ALLOWED_EMAILS` fills in the usual company form, whose button reads **Send my request**, and their company starts as a request: a company on the Free plan, unconfirmed, with its owner's membership `requested`. That membership is the wall: they have no tenant, so every company route refuses them with `no_company`, no AI runs, no workflow starts, and nothing of theirs is public under any address, feed or form. Their screen says "Your request has been sent" and looks again every 30 seconds.

- **On the Overview.** At the very top of **Needs attention**, one row per request, the longest waiting first, up to five, then "N more companies are asking for access", which opens **Customers** on **Waiting for the ITS check**. Each row reads "<Company> is asking for access" with the person's name and email, the kind of business, the size, the city and state, the website and how long they have waited, and **Accept** and **Decline**. A stranger typed all of it, so it is only drawn as text, and the website is a link only as a plain http or https address (`rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow"`, a new tab). The "companies wait for the ITS check" row leaves requests out. While email is not live this row is the only thing that tells anyone someone is waiting.
- **The second press.** **Accept** or **Decline** turns into "Accept <Company>?" with **Yes** and **No**. Then a toast in the API's words, and the row leaves. An error is a toast and the row stays. If someone else got there first, the toast says so and the row refreshes.
- **Accept** (`POST /api/v1/admin/customers/:id/access`, `decision: "accept"`). No reason is typed. They are in, on Free, and it counts as the one-time ITS check: one approval, not two, with the administrator recorded as who confirmed it, whatever `COMPANY_CHECK` says. Their pages can go public from then on. `stuck_role_detector` starts, the owner gets one notice ("Your request was approved", always emailed), and their waiting screen goes to Home by itself. Their history reads "ITS accepted your request for access."
- **Decline** (`decision: "decline"`). No reason is typed. Their screen says "We can't offer access right now" with the ITS inbox, one email says the same, and they cannot send a second request. It is not final: accepting later is the undo, a screen still open goes Home by itself, and their history then has the line "ITS declined your request for access." too. Once a company is accepted, Decline answers 409 `already_accepted`, and **there is no button that takes an accepted company's access away**: **Take back the confirmation** takes its public side offline, and its people can still sign in.
- **On Customers.** A request is under **All** and **Waiting for the ITS check**, on top, with the chip **Asking for access** and the button **Review**. A declined company leaves **All** and every other filter and sits under **Declined**, with the pill **Declined** and the button **Open**; its date and who declined it are the pill's tooltip and on the drawer. Searching under **All** does not reach a declined company. Neither counts as a customer. The sentence above the list, "N companies are waiting for the ITS check", names only the ordinary ones.
- **In the drawer.** Only **Overview** and **ITS check**, and any other `&tab=` lands on the check. **ITS check** holds **Accept** and **Decline** in place of **Confirm the company**, with who is asking, where and how long; for a declined company the date, who, and **Accept** alone. The Overview tab hides **Change plan**, the match score's **Change** and **See the report**. An accepted company gets every tab back at once.
- **One door.** While a company is a request or declined, every other change to it answers 409 `access_request_open`: the ITS check, job boards, how people apply, the trial, the candidate score, the plan and break-glass. Open on purpose: its report (a read), **Sign someone out everywhere** and recording a privacy request.
- **Email.** Email is not live on production yet, so nothing here depends on it, and **no failure raises an alert**, on the person's copy, on ITS's copy ("[access request] <Company> is asking for access"), on the accepted email or on the declined one. A hard bounce from someone who has only asked stops the address as usual and raises nothing about a team member; a spam complaint still raises its alert. Their screen claims an email only when one really left (`settings.accessRequestEmailedAt`, the one thing kept in settings).
- **The other doors.** A platform administrator and an address on `SIGNUP_ALLOWED_EMAILS` go in directly, and the ITS check above applies to them as ever. Going on the list after sending a request changes nothing: accept them. `SIGNUP_MODE=invite` shuts the door again: nobody new can ask, and anyone already waiting keeps their screen and their row, and can still be accepted or declined. A team invitation to another company works for someone waiting or declined, and leaves their own request as it stands.

## How people apply, and the screening allowance

Plan Wave E (founder decisions B1/Q15 and Q17).

- **How people apply** (the customer drawer's Overview, and its **How people apply** tab): the company's default, "Form now, conversation later" unless set (the one-visit flow runs on pilot roles first, founder decision B1; `POST /api/v1/admin/customers/:id/apply-flow`), and each open or draft role's own setting, or the company's default (`PUT /api/v1/roles/:id/apply-mode`). The same role control is on the role's Posting tab for ITS staff: an administrator can always use it, an ITS recruiter only under an active grant for that company that covers the role. The list's **Plan and use** column also shows this month's screening conversations against the allowance. A reason of five characters or more is required and audited. A role whose rule for screening invitations is "Ask me first" or "Only a person" always uses the later path; a change applies to new applicants only. Customers never see or change it.
- **Screening conversations running low**: a card under Customers' **Running low** filter, a chip on the company's row and a row on the Overview, while an alert says a company has used 80% of the month's screening conversations or all of them (`billing.screens_warning`, `billing.screens_used_up`). Nothing is refused: applications are still accepted, anyone part-way through finishes, and once the allowance is used up new applicants get the conversation link by email when more are added. The owners and admins see the same on Today.

## Plans and monthly allowances

Wave S2 (slice F1, 15 October 2026); the plans of 5 November 2026 (`docs/workflow-decisions.md`, PL-1 to PL-13).

- **The plans.** A customer can ask for Free ($0 a month), Basic ($49), Pro ($149) or Ultra ($399), and a new company starts on Free, status active, with no clock. Design Partner is private: ITS gives it and no customer list shows it. Trial, Core, Growth and Company are retired: they are not offered, their rows stay, and a company on one keeps it until someone moves it. Every number is one edit in `packages/db/src/seed.ts`; a company takes a new number only the next time its plan is applied. An existing company with no row for a key is unmetered.
- **Change plan** (the customer drawer's **Plan** tab, under Customers): pick a plan from the active ones, Free, Basic, Pro, Ultra and Design Partner in that order, each with its price and limits in dollars (`GET /api/v1/admin/plans`). A retired plan is not offered, but stays shown and chosen while the company is on it. Give a reason of five characters or more, and confirm with a second press (`POST /api/v1/admin/customers/:id/plan`). It applies at once, is audited as `plan.changed` with the reason and the plans' names (the customer sees it in their own activity history, so write it for them), and settles the allowance alerts the new limits clear. Moving to Design Partner starts the pilot dates. This month's usage is kept: a plan change never hands out a fresh month. The one exception is a monthly AI credit that had no limit, which starts its first month now, so what was spent on Free's credit is not charged to it, and the toast says so ("AI help starts a new month today; other allowances keep their dates."). Any plan but Trial and Design Partner reads as status active.
- **What Change plan does around the plan.** People locked on the company's jobs open, oldest first on each job, up to the new limit (one `application.unlocked` event and audit line each, never a name), and the answer's `unlocked` and the toast say how many: "1 locked candidate opens now." Someone under 60 days old on an open job with no close-out note goes on from the reading, on the new plan's credit, and the interview's link goes by itself. Anyone else is simply visible, with no email and no AI. A move down locks nobody. AI work waiting for credit looks again (`ai.credit_available`), the credit's alerts settle, and what the owner was told about the credit is cleared, so a new plan's lines are told again. Who posts on the boards follows the plan: a move to Pro or Ultra opens ITS's tasks for the boards already waiting, and a move down withdraws them. Owners and admins are told, with the plan's name and how many opened and no dollars (`company.plan_changed`), and every open plan request closes with "Your company is on the <plan> plan now." on its thread; whoever asked and is neither also gets the answer.
- **A company's row and Overview.** **Plan and use** shows the AI credit in dollars ("$1.20 of $10 AI credit", or "$4.00 of $20 AI credit this month, resets December 5"), how many are locked ("3 locked": a count, never who) and the chips **80% of AI credit used**, **AI credit used up** and **Asked to upgrade** (with since when). **Running low** counts a customer whose AI credit is at either line, and `billing.ai_credit_warning` (info) and `billing.ai_credit_used_up` (high) count there, not as alerts waiting for a person; neither is ever critical, and a new plan or a new month resolves them. The Overview tab's job-board line says **Their team posts them** on a plan with no ITS staff time, and the **ITS check** tab hides **Job-board access** for such a company. Dollars stay here: customers read a percentage (founder answer F4).
- **A request to upgrade.** Upgrade sends `POST /api/v1/escalations` with `topic: "plan"`. It reaches the **Queue** as "A customer asked about their plan.", needing "plan and billing", and is never counted against the plan's recruiter requests. Before it is accepted it says "Accept to read what the customer wrote about their plan. It gives you no access to their hiring." Accepting it grants nothing (`grant: null`): the recruiter reads the question and the thread, each opening is audited `escalation.context_viewed` with `{planRequest: true, grant: null}`, and nothing tells the customer about access. A recruiter who is not an administrator reads "An ITS administrator changes the plan in the console. You have no access to this customer's hiring."; an administrator gets **Change the plan in the console**, which opens the **Plan** tab. Reopening one sends it to the queue, never back to the earlier recruiter. While one is open a second is not raised: the customer reads "ITS has your request."
- **Someone locked.** The console, the queue under a grant for that person, privacy requests and the audit trail read locked people as they always did, and a customer's report here counts everyone (`includeLocked`), because ITS is not the paying customer and these must keep working. A candidate who asks for a person, or replies at all, while locked reaches the queue: such a request says **Locked for the company. Do not name them to the company.** (`lockedForCompany` on `GET /api/v1/escalations/:id`), because words cannot be checked by a machine. ITS answers the candidate itself, with **Write to the candidate** on the card in place of "Write to the customer": what goes under **Resolve** is a note only ITS reads, and a later reply from them emails the recruiter holding the request, never their words. Nothing written on the request reaches the team, whose list keeps only that it exists ("An ITS recruiter is helping a candidate you cannot see yet."), and they are told only that a recruiter was brought in, with no name. A grant for a role or the whole company reads nobody locked; a grant naming exactly that application does.
- **Monthly allowances reset**: each allowance starts again from zero when its period ends, on the dates already stored with it; this is worked out when the allowance is read or used, so no job and no migration is involved. Applicants held only because the allowance was used up are let through at the reset by the existing re-check. Active jobs never reset. Settings tells the customer the date ("Monthly allowances reset on October 30.", from `usage[key].resetsAt` on `GET /api/v1/companies/current`).

## The Trial plan's 30 days and 3 people

Founder decision L-02 B (16 October 2026). Since 5 November 2026 Trial is retired and a new company starts on Free, so all of this holds only for a company already on Trial. A company on the Trial plan has 30 days from the day ITS confirms it, and at most 3 people (members of any role, and invitations not yet answered; ITS staff and recruiter grants never count). The dates are on `companies.settings.trial`; there is no migration.

- **Where it shows.** The customers list's **Plan and use** cell and the drawer's **Overview** say "ends Oct 30" or "starts at the ITS check". A trial that has ended shows one caution chip, "ended Oct 15, paused": the date and the pause in one chip, with no second "Trial ended" chip. The drawer's **Overview** adds the people in use ("3 of 3 people"; its tooltip says that invitations not yet answered count too) and each extension with who made it and why.
- **Extend the trial** (the drawer's **Plan** tab, only for a Trial customer whose 30 days have started): a number of days (1 to 90) and a reason of five characters or more, then a second press ("Extend {company}'s trial by 14 days, to November 13?"). The new end counts from the later of the old end and today (`POST /api/v1/admin/customers/:id/trial`). It is audited as `trial.extended` with the reason (the customer sees it in their history), the owners are told, the "trial ended" alert is resolved, and a paused company takes applications again; the email applications held meanwhile are added to their jobs.
- **When a trial ends.** The worker's hourly check raises one alert (`billing.trial_ended`, "{company}'s trial has ended"; Problems › Alerts and the Overview) and tells the owners once; a second run adds nothing. From that moment the company is paused: its jobs take no new applications (candidates read the neutral "not taking new applications right now", never a word about a trial), no new job, chat or reopening starts, and emailed applications are held. Everything already started carries on. The hourly check also gives a confirmed company already on Trial with no dates its 30 days from the first run after this shipped, so nobody is paused the moment it ships.
- **Change plan ends the trial.** Moving a customer off Trial ends it with nothing written over the dates: the alert is resolved, the pause lifts at once, and the email applications held meanwhile are added; the toast says "The trial has ended; new applications are open again." Trial can no longer be chosen in the console (`inactive_plan`).
- **A second company of one's own** goes through ITS (founder decision L-17 A): a person who already belongs to a company, has a request for access waiting or declined, or created one, cannot open another themselves ("To open another company, contact ITS."). A platform administrator can still create a company through the API as before (`POST /api/v1/companies` is not refused for them); people invited into several companies keep the company switcher.

## The six questions for any AI action

Inspecting an AI run must answer, on one screen:

1. **What did the AI see?** Input references (application id, document hashes, packet version, prompt key and version, rendered prompt hash) and, for admins with reason, the rendered prompt.
2. **What did it conclude?** The validated structured output.
3. **Why?** The rationale and evidence fields of the output, and the confidence.
4. **What policy applied?** The resolved policy for the proposed action (level, source: role, company, default, takeover).
5. **What did the human do?** The approval decision or override, with actor, time, and note.
6. **What action occurred?** The resulting events, with links.

One exception, from the founder decision of 9 October 2026 ("hide them until they send"; counsel register item 20): a run about a screening conversation the candidate has not sent (still under way, closed unfinished, sent without it, handed to a person) shows its timings, tokens, status and error, but its structured output is withheld, and the related events say nothing about single answers or how far the candidate got. Nobody at ITS reads answers a candidate never sent (`withoutUnsentRunOutput` in `packages/core/src/screen-answers.ts`). The scorecard of a conversation a recruiter finished on a call is read from the notes alone, and is shown.

## Operational alerts

Failed run rate above threshold; queue age above 5 minutes; adapter health failing; AI error or refusal rate spike; cost per tenant per day anomaly; escalation SLA breach; unmatched inbound emails growing.

How an alert lives (Wave S2, slice F1):

- **Acknowledge** means "seen": while the condition goes on it raises nothing new. An acknowledgement stands for a day at most; after that a condition still going on raises a fresh alert.
- **Resolve** (`POST /api/v1/admin/alerts/:id/resolve`, with an optional note): the problem is dealt with. The alert leaves the console's list, stays in the table for the record, and is audited as `alert.resolved`. The toast says "Resolved. If it happens again, a new alert is raised." A condition that ends on its own (a worker back, a real email provider in use instead of the fake one, an allowance or an AI credit freed by a reset or a plan change) resolves its alert too.
- **Critical alerts are emailed** to every platform administrator, once each, by a job written in the same transaction as the alert (so an alert that rolls back sends nothing); the app's health check sends a stopped worker's alert straight after the commit, since the worker that runs jobs is the thing that is down. The email provider decides whether it really leaves (the fake one in development). Other severities stay in the console only.

## The customer report: what each figure means

Built 30 September 2026 (`docs/spec/21` section 7, P1). The customer sees the figures on a role's Report tab and can download the applicant list and the summary as CSV files (every download is audited as `report.exported`). The administrator sees the same figures for a customer across every role through the **Report** tab of the customer drawer under Customers (`GET /api/v1/admin/customers/:id/report`, audited like every console read). The two views run the same code (`apps/api/src/services/role-report.ts`), so a number on the customer's page and a number in the console can never disagree. Counts and days only: the report itself ranks nobody and carries no match score (those are on the Talent page and the pool board, `docs/spec/22`), and the JSON never carries a name; only the applicant CSV does. Nadine's weekly summary uses these definitions word for word.

**Where the definitions are on screen** (fewer words, 23 October 2026). The cards keep their figures and only their fairness and accountability lines ("People make every decision; the assistant only suggests.", "never held against anyone", "recorded with the person's name and reason", "Counts only; nobody is named here."). What each figure means is one closed disclosure at the end, **What these figures mean** (`ReportDefinitions` in `apps/web/src/pages/RoleReport.tsx`), on the role's Report tab and on the console's Report tab alike. It opens with "Counts and days only; nothing here ranks or scores a candidate." and holds the definitions the cards once carried under their tables: what "typical" and "average" are, what a day is, and what the response time counts. On a role's tab it speaks of "this role"; in the console (`scope="customer"`) of every role ("across all roles"), and it leaves out the last two entries (the must-haves and the AI interview), whose cards are on a role's tab only. Longlist, Shortlist and On hold are the team's private marks; candidates never see them. "Not told yet" counts decisions nobody has told the candidate about, beside those told by message and in person. Identical to the counts on the roles list and the role page. |
| How long each step takes | For each step: how many people are there now and the longest anyone has been there (now minus the moment the step changed); how many people have finished the step and the typical (median) and average number of days they spent in it, from the `application.stage_changed` history. "Typical" is the middle value; "average" adds every stay up and divides. A day is 24 hours from the moment the step changed. "Waiting on you now" is the same measure for the people in the waiting-on-you group. |
| Interviews | Requested (times not yet picked: proposed or scheduling), on the calendar (scheduled), held (completed), the candidate did not show, the interviewer did not show, cancelled, rescheduled by the candidate (the sum of candidate reschedules), and held but not yet decided (completed or no-show with no decision recorded); the same counts per round. |
| Decisions waiting for you | Everything only a person can decide: candidates in the waiting-on-you group, anyone held on an eligibility answer, and pending approvals that are not already counted through their candidate. With the oldest wait in days and the five oldest items. The roles list's "needs you" is the pending-approval count from the same query. |
| Suggestions and decisions | For each assessed candidate, what the assistant suggested (screen, hold, needs a person's look, interview, interview strongly, decline) and the first thing a person did after the suggestion: invited to the AI interview, moved to interview, put on the longlist or shortlist, declined, put on hold, or no decision yet. An eligibility override, a flag or a recorded outcome is not a decision about the suggestion and is not counted here. The assistant never decides. |
| Overruled | Times a person kept someone the eligibility rules would have held, reviewed someone anyway after an eligibility decline, put someone on hold, or flagged an assessment as wrong; from `human.override` events, so each has a name and a reason. |
| Recruiter help | The customer's requests for an ITS recruiter on this role (by role, or by one of the role's candidates; a "Something wrong?" flag on an assessment is one too, plan F11): total, still open (open, claimed or in progress), resolved, past the promised response time (service-level deadline before now and not resolved), and the typical (median) clock hours from the request to the first recruiter response. Response time is clock hours from the request to a recruiter picking it up; the promised time itself counts business hours only, and the definitions say so. |
| Messages | Outbound emails and texts to the role's candidates, by the outbox's own status: sent (sent or delivered), could not be sent (failed or a spam complaint), bounced, delivery unknown (interrupted while sending), waiting to go out (queued or sending); and how many candidates had a message that did not reach them. |
| Outcomes | The current record per candidate (a corrected record is replaced by its correction): hired, withdrew, declined by the employer, declined the offer, closed without a hire; how many are in the talent pool (never counting someone who asked not to be kept for future roles); how many records were corrected; the role's own outcome once it is closed; and the reasons candidates were declined, from the decline reason a person chose. Always shown under the statement that any offer happened outside Recruiting OS. |
| The must-haves (the card "How the pool meets the must-haves") | One role only (the role's Report tab; not the console's across-roles view): for each must-have, how many of the people read so far show it, partly show it, show in their own words that they do not meet it (a checked quote only), and say nothing either way (someone read before a must-have was added is left out of its count); how many show every must-have; and the gentle note when one must-have is not shown by 60% or more of at least five people ("stricter than the job needs?"), which never turns anyone down. From the same findings as the match score and the pool board (`docs/spec/22`). Counts only, no names. |
| The AI interview | One role only (the role's Report tab; not the console's across-roles view): how many people were offered the AI interview, started it and finished it, how many have it open now, the typical (median) time to finish, from the first answer to sending, how many chose a person instead (asked to be contacted, or sent their application without it), and the questions people chose not to answer in the answers they sent, with how many chose not to answer each. From each person's latest conversation. Counts only, never a name or an answer, and nothing from a conversation nobody sent, so not where anyone stopped (founder decision of 9 October 2026). Choosing a person or not answering a question is never held against anyone. |

The applicant CSV has one row per application: applied on, name, source, source detail, stage, days in stage, recommendation, decision (the latest thing a person did; a decision after an interview reads as "Not moving forward after interview", "Moved to the next round" or "Finalist"), outcome, interviews, interviews completed. It carries no AI rationale, evidence, résumé text or pay. An ITS recruiter working under a grant that names one candidate gets that candidate's row alone, the same limit the role's applications list applies.
