# 16. Readiness checklist, in plain language, for Nadine

Written 21 September 2026 by the production team; revised five times the same day after a three-reader panel (a
non-technical founder's view, a lawyer's view, an operations view). This is the founder's version of the readiness
register (`docs/readiness/01`, section E and the tables it points to). **Nothing here is approved yet.** Each gate stays
open until you, counsel, or the named person completes it and the evidence exists.

This is not legal advice. Counsel advises; ITS decides and is responsible. Where a gate says counsel must approve, the
team's wording describes the question, never the answer. Counsel is **not yet engaged** (the counsel register says so),
which is why gate 1.7 exists.

How to read each gate:

- **What it means** in one or two sentences.
- **Why it matters**: what goes wrong if it is skipped.
- **Who completes it**: you, Paul, counsel, or the production team (Claude, working under your approval).
- **Evidence**: the record that proves it, and where it is filed.
- **You decide?** whether the gate needs a decision from you (choose, accept, adopt, or record).
- **Counsel approves?** whether counsel's written answer must be recorded before the gate closes.
- **Needs a real staging account?** whether it can only be finished once the real accounts exist.
- **Today**: where it stands in the register right now, using the register's six words (plus its own note "in progress
  by you and Paul" where the register says so).

The register uses six statuses: *Not started*, *Built with mocks* (works on a laptop with fake services), *Verified in
staging* (works on the real services in the private copy), *Counsel approved*, *Ready for production*, and *Blocked*
(waiting on a decision, an account or an answer that does not exist yet). Nothing built against a fake service is ever called ready for production. If you would rather read one short block per gate than these wide tables, say so and the team will reformat it.

Stage 2 is numbered 2.0 to 2.8 with no 2.3 (a gate was merged during revision); the count is 56 gates. Gates that come from the register carry no label. Gates the team added because the register or another document
implied them are marked **(team addition)** so you can tell the two apart.

Where things are written down: your decisions are recorded in `docs/spec/00` section M (M1 is the 45-day privacy
deadline, M2 texting off, M3 Microsoft Calendar deferred; D22 in section G is the later job-runner migration); the team's
execution log is section N; your sign-off table is section O; counsel's answers and every approval are recorded against
the numbered items of the counsel register, `docs/spec/11`. "Marked approved" below always means that record exists.

A few terms, once:

- *production*: the live copy of the product that real customers and candidates use; *staging* is the private rehearsal copy.
- *region*: which country's data centre an account uses.
- *data-processing terms*: a vendor's written promise about what it may do with the data we give it.
- *a journey*: a scripted walk through the product as one kind of user (a candidate, an owner).
- *a screen reader* (VoiceOver on Apple devices) reads the screen aloud for people who cannot see it; *assistive technology* is the general term.
- *job feeds*: the machine-readable lists of open roles that job sites read; *the queue preview*: what an ITS recruiter sees about a request before accepting it (a generic summary, never the candidate's words).
- *Twilio*: the text-message sending service; *a job runner*: a service that runs scheduled background work.
- *the consent screen*: the Google page that asks an interviewer to allow calendar access.
- *design partner*: a first customer who agrees to use the product early, with made-up candidates until stage 4 closes,
  in exchange for shaping it.
- *second factor* (also MFA): a code from your phone in addition to the password.
- *DNS* and the *zone file*: the address book that points `identifiedtalent.com` at the right mail and web servers, and
  the saved copy of it.
- *staging* and a *preview address*: a private copy of the product on the real services, and the web address it answers
  on, used for checking before any customer sees it.
- *the repository*: where the code is stored (on GitHub once that account exists).
- *the console*: the internal administrator screen inside the product.
- *the vault*: the 1Password password manager where people keep secrets and, with a named folder, contracts. *The
  platform's secret store* is different: the locked place inside the database (Supabase Vault) where the software keeps
  connection secrets.
- *the ledger*: the record in the database that says who is an administrator.
- *audited*: written to a permanent record of who did what and when, which the product's own accounts cannot edit or delete.
- *rotate a secret*: replace it with a new one and retire the old, following the written steps.
- *a token* here means a sign-in code or link; *a complaint* means a person marked our email as spam.
- *exhibits*: the attachments that form part of a signed agreement; the *incident-notification window* is how many hours ITS has to tell a partner after a data incident.
- *a penetration test*: paying an outside specialist to try to break in, with permission, and fix what they find.
- *row-level security*: the database rule that keeps one customer's rows invisible to another.
- *break-glass*: an administrator's short, recorded emergency access to one customer's data.
- *the worker process*: the background program that sends reminders and runs timers.
- *the automatic checks*: the tests and scans that run on every change before it can be used (footnote 1 lists them).
- *a sign-in registration*: the record kept with Microsoft (for staff) or Google (for customers) that lets people sign
  in to our product; *the calendar registration* is the separate record with Google that lets an interviewer connect a
  calendar.
- *keyed references*: identifiers scrambled with a secret so an outside service cannot tell who a person is.
- *suppresses an address*: stops sending email to it.
- *domain records*: the DNS entries that let our email provider send mail as `identifiedtalent.com`.
- *point-in-time recovery* and a *scratch project*: the paid database option that can restore to any minute, and the
  throw-away copy a restore is practised into.
- *anonymization*: removing everything that identifies a person from a record while keeping the record of what happened.
- *subprocessor*: any outside service that touches candidate data on our behalf.
- *A2P 10DLC*: the carriers' registration a business needs before it may text people.
- *browser-protection policy*: tells browsers to run only scripts we wrote, so that someone cannot smuggle their own
  script into our pages (*script injection*); first in "report only" (browsers tell us what they would have blocked),
  later in "enforce". The switch is a setting called `CSP_MODE` on the staging and production sites.
- *evaluation suites*: scripted test conversations that check the AI's answers against a passing score; a failure would
  mean a candidate could get a wrong answer.
- *a request limit*: how many times one person or program may hit the site in a minute; it stops password guessing and
  scraping. The counter must be shared across servers or it is easy to get around.
