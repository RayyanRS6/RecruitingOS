Prepared 30 September 2026 by the production team as input for counsel and the founder. ## D. Service description and limitations (new)

D.1 The Service is described in Exhibit A, which also states what is not included in release 1 and what the assistant never does. Exhibit A is part of this Agreement. Customer acknowledges that it has read Exhibit A and is not relying on any feature not described there.

D.2 **No automated decisions.** The Service does not reject candidates, does not decide who to hire, does not negotiate or send offers, and does not take adverse action. It shows each candidate a match score from 0 to 100, worked out by the Service's software from evidence checked against the candidate's own application (never written by the AI), with the breakdown of how the score was reached shown to Customer's users; information a candidate did not give is left out of the score rather than counted against them. Customer's users may sort candidates by the score and may apply the Service's suggested groupings (shortlist, longlist, not moving forward), but only a person applies them, each decision is private until a person tells the candidate, and the Service records who decided and why. Customer's users make every hiring decision. This restates pilot section 6 and is enforced in the Service's code; it cannot be switched off by either party's settings. [Counsel: whether a visible score and a list sorted by it make the Service an automated employment decision tool under New York City Local Law 144 and similar laws, and what bias audit and notice follow; docs/spec/11-counsel-register.md.]

D.3 **Manual job distribution.** The Service has no connection to LinkedIn, Indeed or ZipRecruiter. ITS staff post, pause and remove Customer's postings on those boards by hand under Customer's own accounts (section H). Availability, placement and visibility of a posting on a board are controlled by the board, not by ITS.

D.4 **No guarantee of results.** ITS does not guarantee any number of applicants, any candidate quality, any time to hire, or any hire. The Service organizes information and ITS recruiters advise; hiring results depend on Customer's role, pay, market and decisions.

D.5 **Scope.** The Service is offered for roles located in the United States, in English, and for candidates in the United States. Customer will not use it for roles or candidates elsewhere without written agreement.

D.6 **Changes.** ITS may add features and may change how a feature works, and will tell Customer of material changes in writing. ITS will not remove a capability listed in Exhibit A during the Initial Term without Customer's agreement, except where required by law or by a provider's terms.

D.7 **Release status.** Customer acknowledges the honest statements in Exhibit A: release 1 is new, its capabilities were built and tested against simulated services and are verified on live services before Customer's first candidate, and Customer receives the acceptance record.

## F. Compliance and roles of the parties (new, complements pilot section 11)

F.1 Customer is the employer. Customer is responsible for the lawfulness of its job requirements, eligibility questions, pay ranges and hiring decisions, and for any notice or record-keeping duty that applies to it as an employer, including under federal, state and local employment, anti-discrimination, pay-transparency and automated-decision laws.

F.2 The Service supports compliance by displaying the pay range where a state requires it, limiting eligibility questions to lawful job requirements, displaying an AI-use notice and a privacy notice to every candidate, recording every human decision with a reason, and never declining a candidate automatically. These supports are not legal advice and do not shift Customer's responsibility.

F.3 ITS is responsible for operating the Service as described in Exhibits A and B and for its own staff's conduct under Exhibit D-2.

## G. Data-processing terms (new; replaces the bracketed parts of pilot section 5)

G.1 **Roles.** Customer determines the purposes of processing candidate data and is the business (or controller) for it. ITS processes candidate data on Customer's behalf as a service provider (or processor) to provide the Service, and for no other purpose. [Counsel: confirm the CCPA/CPRA service-provider language and whether Customer meets the "business" thresholds; confirm whether a separate data-processing addendum is required.]

G.2 **Instructions.** Customer's instructions are this Agreement, Customer's settings in the Service, and Customer's users' actions in the Service. ITS will tell Customer if an instruction appears to conflict with the law.

G.3 **Categories of data and data subjects.** Candidates for Customer's roles and Customer's users. Candidate data: name, email, phone (optional), location (optional), resume or written summary, eligibility answers, screening answers, interview scheduling choices, accommodation requests, messages, and outcomes recorded by Customer. The Service collects no Social Security numbers, dates of birth, government identification, bank details, health information, background-check results or tax information. [Resolved from the Service's data model.]

G.4 **Purpose limitation.** ITS uses candidate data only to consider the candidate for the role applied to and, unless the candidate opts out, for future roles at Customer; to communicate with the candidate; to schedule interviews; to keep records the law requires; and to provide recruiter assistance Customer requests. ITS does not sell candidate data, does not share it with other customers, does not pool it across customers, and does not use it to train AI models. ITS's AI provider does not train on data sent through its commercial API. [Resolved from the Service's design and the provider's commercial terms; counsel to confirm the provider's terms once the account exists.]

G.5 **Confidentiality.** ITS staff who may access candidate data are bound by confidentiality and access it only under Exhibit D-2.

G.6 **Security.** ITS maintains the measures in Exhibit B (section E).

G.7 **Subprocessors.** ITS uses the subprocessors in Exhibit C, each under written terms that restrict its use of the data to its function. ITS will give Customer [30] days' written notice before adding a subprocessor that will process candidate data; Customer may object in writing within that period, and if the parties cannot resolve the objection Customer may end the Agreement on notice without penalty. The current operational register is maintained by ITS and reflected in Exhibit C. [Note for the founder: Exhibit C cannot be true until the accounts exist; a malware-scanning provider for resumes, if a hosted one is chosen, is an addition.]

G.8 **Candidate requests.** ITS handles candidate requests for access, correction, deletion, human review and withdrawal through the process in Exhibit D: acknowledgment within one business day, a tracked deadline of [45] days (extendable once with notice), verification of the requester, fulfilment recorded and confirmed to the candidate. Customer sees that a request of a given kind exists and was fulfilled, not its content. Deletion is performed by anonymization where a retention duty may apply, only after a recorded decision by counsel and an administrator's confirmation, and never automatically. Requests concerning a candidate Customer hired are referred to counsel because the employment record belongs to Customer. Customer will forward any request it receives directly to privacy@identifiedtalent.com within one business day. [The acknowledgment period, the 45-day deadline and the anonymization approach are the Service's implemented values, proposed pending counsel.]

G.9 **Retention, return and deletion.** ITS retains candidate data for the periods in Exhibit E (proposed: four years from the last activity for candidate records, seven years for audit and privacy-request records, sooner on a fulfilled candidate request), subject to litigation holds and to a hired candidate's employment records belonging to Customer. No automatic deletion runs until counsel approves the schedule. At the end of the Agreement ITS will, at Customer's written choice within [30] days, (a) provide Customer an export of its candidate data in a common format [counsel: confirm what "return" means for a service provider; the Service exports one candidate's data per request today, and a customer-wide export is not yet built], or (b) retain it for the remaining retention period and then delete or anonymize it, or (c) delete or anonymize it sooner where no retention duty applies, in each case confirming in writing.

G.10 **Incident notification.** ITS will notify Customer's owner and admins of a confirmed incident involving Customer's candidate data within [one business day of confirming it] [counsel: choose between one business day and 72 hours; the incident plan commits to one business day], with what was affected, whose data, the period, what ITS did and what Customer should do, and will cooperate with Customer's own notifications. ITS engages counsel before any notice to candidates or a regulator. A written post-incident note follows within five business days.

G.11 **Records and audit.** The Service keeps an unalterable record of every ITS access to Customer's data, every human decision, every change to Customer's team, settings and notices, every use of a calendar credential, every privacy request and every message to a candidate. Customer can see recruiter access in the Service and the timeline of each candidate; ITS provides other records relating to Customer on written request within [ten] business days. [A customer-wide audit export is not yet built.]

G.12 **Location.** All processing takes place in the United States. ITS will not transfer candidate data outside the United States without Customer's written agreement.

G.13 **Assistance.** ITS assists Customer, at no extra charge during the program, with candidate requests, with responding to a regulator's inquiry about the Service, and with any assessment Customer must perform about its use of automated tools, to the extent the information is about the Service.

## I. Customer responsibilities (new, extends pilot section 4)

Customer will: (a) ensure each of its users signs in with a work account it controls, with two-step verification on that account; (b) give each user the smallest role that does their job and tell ITS the same day a user should be removed; (c) provide accurate information about its company and roles; (d) make every decision to advance, hold or decline a candidate through its own users and record outcomes in the Service; (e) not enter into the Service any category of data the Service is designed not to collect (section G.3); (f) provide honest feedback at the weekly meeting; (g) not resell or share the Service.
