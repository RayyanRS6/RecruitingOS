# 07. UX and design system

## The experience in one line

Tell us who you need. We run the process. You decide.

## Owner / Hiring Manager Mode

Primary navigation (docs/FOUNDER-CONTEXT.md §14): **Today, Roles, Talent, Interviews, Offers, Insights**; secondary: Activity, Recruiter help, Integrations, Settings. Views appear as their slices land (Today, Roles, Talent and Setup today; Interviews with Slice 3, Offers with Slice 4, Insights with Slice 9), never as empty menu items.

**Simple screens (Wave S1, 14 October 2026).** On screen the sections are named **Home** (was Today), **Jobs** (was Roles), **Candidates** (was Talent), **Interviews** and **Settings** (was Setup); the addresses did not change (`/`, `/roles`, `/talent`, `/interviews`, `/setup`). Each screen has one heading, at most one short line under it and one main action; explanations and rarely used controls sit one click away, in a tab, a **More** menu or a `details.more` block ("Why", "How this works", "Show more"), never removed. The words on screen follow one glossary: job (not role), job details and version N (not packet), the job builder (not intake), screening chat (not screen or screening conversation), AI review (not reading), All matches (not Any reading). The sections below keep their earlier names where they describe the design history.
- *Home*: the greeting and one line ("2 things need you" / "All caught up"); **Needs you** as short rows (title, one line, its buttons, the rest under **Why**), five shown, then "Show N more"; **Your jobs** (one row per job with its status and counts); the **Who are you hiring?** box; the assistant's log folded as **Recent activity (N)**.
- *A job*: one header on `/roles/:id` and `/roles/:id/talent` (eyebrow "Job · company", title, status pill, one facts line; **Bring in a recruiter** and a **More** menu with pause, accepting applications, close, the closing message, hire again, candidate view and edit). One tab strip under it, Candidates first: **Candidates (N) · Job · Scoring · Interview plan · Share · Report · Activity**. A live job opens on its Candidates from the rail, Jobs and Home. Old `?tab=` names (overview, requirements, evaluation, posting, history) still land.
- *Candidates*: the stage strip always shows All, New, Shortlist, Interviewing, Hired and Not moving forward, plus any stage someone is at; the other stages wait under **More stages**. At most two chips per row besides the score and the stage button.
- *The candidate panel's Overview*: decisions waiting first, then the verdict (score, band, confidence, one line), the suggested next step, up to three strengths and three concerns, then **Full breakdown** (how the score was worked out, the AI review with every quote, eligibility answers, "Something wrong?"). Contact is one line; History keeps every event.
- *Settings*: five tabs, **Company · Team · Notifications · Hiring rules · Security** (`?tab=`); every old `#setup-…` link opens the right tab and scrolls to its card.
- *Private decisions*: wherever a screen says a decision is private, it also says in one line that the standard message goes by itself after 5 working days if nobody sends one, with the date when it is known (founder decision D-03 A).

**More of the Kanvis-like flow (Wave S2, 14-15 October 2026).** Built in our own words and code, with no new library:
- *Jobs list* (`/roles`): heading "Jobs" with **Start a hire**; status chips with counts (**All · Live · Drafts · Paused · Closed**), **Search by title**, and a sort (**Recent activity**, Newest first, Oldest first, Title A to Z), all kept in the address (`?status=&sort=&q=`). Each row: title, status pill, one line of facts and small badges (**N to review**, **N new**, **Draft** with its age, **Not live**, **No applicants yet**); a live job's row opens its Candidates, with **Post on LinkedIn** for the people who may share it. Each filter that finds nothing has its own empty state with one button.
- *Quick start* (`/roles/new?way=quick`): the new-job page offers two ways, **Answer a few quick questions** (the main button) or describe the job (the job builder's conversation, unchanged). Ten steps, one question per screen with "Step N of 10": title (or paste an ad to fill it in), employment type, where, minimum experience, pay (always shown on our ads), closing date, must-have skills, deal-breakers, screening questions, then a review with **Edit** on each row and **Write the job post**, which hands everything to the existing job builder and its fairness check. Enter moves on; the answers survive a reload in this browser; **Start over** clears them. Suggestions come from our hiring guide when it knows the job; for any other job (Wave S3) the assistant suggests must-haves and yes/no questions from the answers so far ("Suggesting skills from your answers…" while it works, nothing extra if it fails): must-haves unticked and labelled "AI suggestion", questions ticked after the general ones when they arrive while the questions step is open, unticked if they arrive after the person moved on.
- *Post on LinkedIn* (the Share tab, `#linkedin`): three tones (**Proud**, **Straight**, **Friendly**), text made from the job's own facts and its LinkedIn tracked link, an editable box with a live preview and a character count, **Copy and open LinkedIn** and **Copy text**. No AI, no LinkedIn connection; nothing is posted for anyone. Only for a live job whose company ITS has confirmed.
- *Application progress line* (the candidate's status page): **Applied → Screening chat** (only when the job uses one) **→ Review → Interview → Decision**, past steps ticked and the current one marked, with **What happens next** in one line under it. Server-rendered with no script; messages, withdrawal, privacy and every link stay below.

**Today** answers four questions in one line and then in cards: what needs my attention, who are the best people to look at, what is at risk, what did the system accomplish since I last checked. It opens to the morning brief, a sequence of cards, each with a plain-language headline and one or two actions:

| Brief section | Card | Primary action | Secondary |
|---|---|---|---|
| New applicants | "12 people applied to Dental Hygienist since yesterday. 4 look strong." | Review the 4 | Show all |
| Top candidates | Candidate card: name, one-line summary, two strengths, one concern, confidence, evidence source chips | Interview | Pass · Ask a recruiter |
| Candidates needing review | "3 applicants need a human look (unclear license status)." | Review | |
| Interviews today | "Maria R., 2:00pm with you and Dr. Chen. Brief attached." | Open brief | Reschedule |
| Decisions needed | Approval card: "Approve the offer to Maria: $52/hr, starts Oct 6." | Approve | Edit · Ask a recruiter |
| Offers | "Offer to Jonah sent Monday, viewed twice, no response." | Nudge | Call him (recruiter) |
| Candidates at risk | "Two strong candidates have waited 4 days for a decision." | Decide now | |
| Roles stuck | "Front Office Coordinator: 5 screened, no interviews scheduled in 6 days." | Schedule them | Have a recruiter review |
| Upcoming starts | "Maria starts Monday. Two things need you." | Open checklist | |

Below the brief, people who may start a hire see **Who are you hiring?**: what they type (a sentence, or a whole job ad) opens the job builder on a new draft, and each of their drafts in progress shows as a card with Continue and Discard. Every action that requires approval is a card, never a sentence that pretends it happened.

**Job builder** (`/roles/new`, plan Wave C; reached from Today, **Start a hire** in the rail and Roles, never a new menu section). A split view: the conversation on the left, the draft on the right (one at a time on a phone, with a switch). The assistant asks with up to three tap-to-answer question cards docked where the message box is (pick one, pick any, team members, the pay card), each with "Why we ask"; the picks go back into the conversation as one plain summary. The draft fills in as the person answers; each section says who sees it (candidates, asked of candidates, used to review applications but not on the job page, or only the team). "Write the draft" shows the real steps as the server records them. After that, typed words ask for a change, shown field by field and saved only on Confirm. Wording the fairness check flags waits for a person: change it, or keep it with a reason the approver sees. "Ask for approval" stays greyed, listing in the server's words what is missing, until the draft is written, up to date with the answers, and complete. When the assistant fails, "Bring in a recruiter" hands over the draft and its conversation.

**Roles.** One row per role: title, days open, a five-segment pipeline bar (applied · screened · interviewing · deciding · offer), and "what needs you." Drafts in progress are listed above the roles (owners and admins also see the team's, to pick one up). Opening a role shows the packet summary, the pipeline, and the posting status per channel with plain-language status ("Live on your careers page and Google. Indeed: post it from your account and forward applications to this address" with a copy button).

**Talent.** For a role: a list of candidates grouped by "waiting on you," "in process," "not moving forward." Each row: name, overall (Strong match / Potential match / Weak match / Insufficient evidence), two strengths, one concern, confidence. "Not moving forward" is the exclusion view: reason, evidence, policy applied, and "Review anyway" for anything an eligibility rule declined. Nothing is hidden. Compare mode: up to five candidates side by side by must-have and competency with evidence expandable, each with their match score, in the order chosen or highest score first. Candidates get a 0-100 match score worked out by code from checked evidence against the role's must-haves (never written by the AI), with its breakdown of what cost points one click away wherever the number shows, and with missing information set aside rather than counted against them; a person always decides. The score's bands have their own words (Very high, High, Medium, Low) so they are never confused with the AI's overall reading above. The list can be sorted by score, highest first (or newest, waiting longest, name). Above the list, the stage strip counts everyone at each of the nine stages: New, Longlist, Shortlist, Interviewing, Cancelled, On hold, Offer, Hired, Not moving forward. **Cancelled** (founder decisions 1A to 4A) holds the candidates who cancelled a booked interview and the ones who did not come to one; while it is chosen, three chips split it by what each person chose ("Asked for new times", "No longer interested", "No-show"), each row says where their new times stand ("New times go out automatically Thu, Oct 15", "Getting new times ready", "Waiting for you"), and an alert offers **Send new times…** for several people at once.

**The pool board** (a view inside Talent, not a sixth page; stacked sections with counts on phones): three columns in our words, Shortlist, Longlist and Not moving forward, placed by code from each person's evidence (`packages/core/src/board.ts`): a high or very high score read with confidence and every must-have known goes to the Shortlist; a medium score to the Longlist; a low score or a must-have the candidate's own words show is missing to Not moving forward; anyone who needs a closer look (a résumé reading or a scorecard that says too little, at any stage), was read with low confidence, has no reading, has an unreadable résumé, has a must-have said to be missing with nothing quoted, has a must-have not yet known on a strong reading, or is held on an eligibility answer goes to the Longlist with a **Needs your eye** line giving the reason. A suggested decline never starts ticked on thin or moving ground: a low score with must-haves not yet known or from under half the requirements, an open screening conversation whose answers would count most, a reading made against an older version of the role whose must-haves have changed since, or a reading that itself suggested a step forward; nor does a Shortlist place when the reading itself suggested holding back. People still applying sit apart; people already decided, sorted or interviewing show where they are, marked done. The column lines under the titles are "Good evidence on the must-haves", "Some evidence, or more to find out" and "A must-have shown missing, or a low score on what is known". Each card: score (band word and number) with the coverage beside it, a state chip (**Ready to apply** in plain grey when Review and apply will start with them ticked, amber **Needs your eye**, red **Missing must-have** only on a checked quote, or **Done**), the reading's own confidence as a separate chip, a one-line reason, a line of small facts (the must-have line "Must-haves: 3 of 4 shown, 1 not yet known", what the score rests on, "Read against version 1 of the role…", "Invited to the screening conversation automatically", "A suggestion is waiting for you"), two strengths and the costliest gap with where each came from, and a suggested next step in our words (Interview, Short phone call, Ask for more detail, Keep as backup, Read it yourself, Check eligibility, Not moving forward). The header counts the people still to sort ("To sort: 2 shortlist · 1 longlist · 1 not moving forward · 3 already sorted or decided"); someone hired or interviewing is never counted as "on the shortlist". Above the columns, the summary in fixed sentences built from the records (who is still to sort, the must-have shown least often, who has waited longest, up to three next steps naming people, and the gentle "stricter than needed?" note, which also sits on the role's Report tab with the per-must-have counts). **Review and apply** opens from the board: three groups with each person's reason and score, confident placements ticked and the rest unticked with the reason, a live counter ("5 decisions ready: 2 shortlist · 1 longlist · 2 not moving forward"), a reason per not-moving-forward pre-filled from that person's evidence and editable, and "Apply N decisions"; the line under the title says "Untick anyone you want to decide yourself, change a reason if needed, then apply"; the not-moving-forward group says nobody is emailed now, with a reminder to send the decision message from the Talent page, and names the safety net (founder decision Q27: if nobody tells them within five working days the standard message goes by itself, or, when the settings ask for approval or keep these messages with a person, a recruiter is brought in and nothing is sent); the toast says "Filed privately. Nobody was emailed." One-click questions sit beside the board (who is missing a must-have and why, strongest evidence for a must-have, who might I be overlooking, compare these); each answer filters the board and shows its quotes and what it looked at.

**Calendar.** Interviews this week with briefs, interviewer load, and unscheduled interviews waiting on candidates.

**Setup** (on screen **Settings**, in five tabs since Wave S1). Company profile, team and roles, integrations with status badges, "What the AI may do on its own" (the policy editor, grouped by lifecycle stage with the four levels as a segmented control that offers only the levels an action allows), notices and consent texts (with the jurisdictions they apply to), billing and usage.

**Bring in a recruiter** is a persistent button in the conversation and on every decision card. It opens a short sheet: "What do you want help with?" with three quick picks (Review these candidates · Help me decide · Take this role over) and a free-text box, says what the recruiter will see and until when, then confirms the SLA. Opened from the top of Today (no role or candidate in view), it first asks "Which hire is this about?" (a role, or All of your hiring) and needs a one-line note.

## Recruiter Mode

A permission set that adds routes and density:

- **Pipeline table** per role with stage columns, filters, bulk actions (invite to screen, send message, move, reject with reason), and keyboard navigation.
- **Search** across the tenant's candidates and documents (full text plus similarity), with saved searches.
- **Candidate workspace**: timeline of every event, all assessments with evidence, screen transcript, interviews and scorecards, communications thread with reply, notes, tasks, compensation, and the audit strip.
- **Scorecards and rubric editing** on the packet (creates a new packet version).
- **Sequences and communication controls**: templates, sending windows, channel rules.
- **Workflow controls**: view runs for a role, pause, retry a step, override with reason, take over.
- **Multi-client switching** for ITS recruiters, with the escalation queue as their home.

Recruiter Mode never shows a candidate anything the owner cannot see; it shows more of the machinery.

## Candidate experience

- **Job page**: title, pay range (shown when the packet allows; required by law in some states, handled by the compliance layer), location, schedule, a plain description, "what to expect" with the steps and typical timeline, the AI disclosure, and one **Apply now** button (twice on a wide screen: by the title and in the panel). It is a pill edged in the field-border grey: a dark circle with an arrow beside the words; on a mouse's hover or a keyboard's focus a dark fill slides across the words in 200 ms and the words turn light as it passes them (transform only, no blur; founder decision Q2, 10 October 2026). On a phone it rests as circle and words; on Android pressing fills it, on iOS Safari there is no press effect because the page carries no script. It is the only button of its kind: the careers page's View and apply, the form's Send my application and every button in the application, Start a hire included, stay as they are.
- **Apply**: name, email, phone (with a separate, unchecked SMS consent box), resume upload or four short questions, the eligibility questions in plain language, the state-appropriate notices, and a confirmation with a status link. The form alone under three minutes on a phone; on a pilot role, with the written conversation that follows it in the same visit, about fifteen, and the pages say so.
- **Screening conversation** (candidate, server-rendered, no script): the AI line on top of every page; a fixed introduction with Start, Do this later and I'd rather talk to a person; one question at a time in large type on the white sheet, with "Question 2 of 5" over a thin native progress bar; the candidate's answers as their own near-black messages and the assistant's follow-ups on the left; tap answers for factual questions; a quiet "Skip this question"; "Check your answers" with Change on each, then Send; the person choice as two equal outlined buttons.
- **Screen**: text or web chat, one question at a time, progress ("3 of 7"), pause and resume, "talk to a person" always visible.
- **Schedule**: tap a slot; confirmation with calendar file; reschedule and cancel links.
- **Interview prep**: who they will meet, format, length, what to bring, the case prompt if any.
- **Status**: the progress line first (Applied, Screening chat, Review, Interview, Decision; Wave S2), then what happens next in one line and when they will hear; withdrawal in one tap.
- **Offer**: the letter, a place to ask questions, signature, a countdown to day one.
- **Privacy**: a page explaining what is stored, for how long, and how to request access or deletion.

Tone: warm, direct, specific. Signed by the company's hiring team, "assisted by an AI recruiter." Never "I" from the AI without the disclosure line present.

**The same look as the application.** The candidate pages are server-rendered by the API (`apps/api/src/pages/layout.tsx`) and cannot load the application's stylesheet, so their inline stylesheet carries a copy of the token values in `packages/ui/src/tokens.css`, in both themes. `apps/api/src/candidate-look.test.ts` fails if the two drift, so changing a colour in `tokens.css` means changing the copy in `layout.tsx` too. The buttons follow the same rules (a near-black primary, outlined secondary ones), with small uppercase eyebrows and pills for a job's facts. The fonts (IBM Plex Sans 400 and 600, Bricolage Grotesque 700) are asked for only from the deployment's own origin (`/fonts/`, served from `apps/shell/public/fonts`), so the pages' content-security policy names no outside host and no font host learns who opened a job page. The files are put there by `scripts/fetch-fonts.ts`, which pins exact package versions and records a SHA-256 for every file in `apps/api/src/pages/fonts.lock.json`; until they are committed the pages use the device's own sans-serif. Their only motion is the press of a button and the Apply now fill; hover fills show only where there is a mouse, so a tapped button never stays grey; with reduced motion asked for, transitions and animations stop, pseudo-elements included.

## Voice (docs/FOUNDER-CONTEXT.md §18)

One personality, the recruiting chief of staff: concise and decisive for employers ("Three candidates need your review. I recommend advancing two. Here is why."), collaborative and evidence-based with hiring managers, warm and specific with candidates, transparent everywhere. Never: "perfect fit", "culture fit" without a definition, "the algorithm says", "rejected by AI", "human capital", "resource", "rockstar", "ninja", or anything that reduces a person to a score (wherever the match score appears, on a row, a card or the candidate panel, its breakdown and the evidence behind it are one click away).

## Design system (`packages/ui`)

**Intent.** Modern, trustworthy, premium, extremely simple: a quiet, paper-like workspace where the words and the people stand out, not the chrome. Not a legacy ATS: no dense grey grids by default, no stage-name jargon in Owner Mode. The product is calm; urgency is communicated by placement (top of the brief), not by alarm colours. The founder chose this look in October 2026 (a warm off-white ground, white cards with hairline borders, near-black type and buttons); it is our own identity, with our own names, wording and wordmark, and no illustrations. The dark theme is cool rather than warm: the same roles on a neutral, Obsidian-like near-black and grey with no warm tint, while the light theme keeps the warm paper (founder decision Q1, 10 October 2026). From Obsidian UI we took ideas, never its code or libraries: calm normal-case table headings on a faint band, a row tint under the mouse, figures right-aligned, a slight press on buttons, and a sliding-arrow pill on Apply now.

**Palette.** Everything lives in `packages/ui/src/tokens.css` as CSS custom properties, with a light and a dark theme. The dark theme follows the device setting (`prefers-color-scheme`) unless the page sets `data-theme="light"` or `data-theme="dark"` on `<html>`. Each colour's contrast ratio is written beside it, and `tokens.test.ts` recomputes the important pairs in both themes so a change cannot quietly drop below WCAG AA. In dark mode the page is `#0e0e10` and cards `#18181b`, the greys in plain steps with the faintest blue cast and no brown; the soft status fills are each status colour laid at 14-15% over the card grey. `tokens.test.ts` also checks that the light greys stay warm (red above blue) and the dark ones cool (blue at least equal to red), and that `--tint` sits between `--surface` and `--surface-2`.

| Role | Token | Light | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | `--ground` | warm off-white `#f7f5f0` | the page |
| Surface | `--surface` | white | cards, lists, the drawer header, menus |
| Sunken | `--surface-2`, `--surface-3` | warm greys (cool greys in dark) | neutral chips, hover, the grey score badge |
| Tint | `--tint` | a half-step between white and the sunken grey | the band behind table headings and the row under the mouse |
| Rail | `--surface-rail` | a touch lighter than the ground | the left rail |
| Hairline | `--line`, `--line-2` | warm light greys (cool in dark) | borders between surfaces; outlines of secondary buttons and pills |
| Field border | `--line-strong` | `#8c8578` (3.7:1) | inputs, selects and text areas, which must be seen (WCAG 1.4.11) |
| Ink | `--ink`, `--ink-2`, `--ink-3` | near-black, dark grey, muted grey (5.5:1 at the lightest) | text: titles, body, secondary lines |
| Primary | `--primary`, `--primary-hover`, `--on-primary` | near-black with white text | the one main action on a screen; in dark mode it turns light with dark text |
| Links and focus | `--link`, `--focus` (`--accent-ink`, `--accent`) | cobalt | links and focus rings only, nothing else |
| Good | `--good`, `--good-soft`, `--good-line`, `--good-dot`, `--good-solid` | green | positive, live, match score 70 or more, the solid "Hired" pill |
| Caution | `--caution`, `--caution-soft`, `--caution-line`, `--caution-dot` | amber | middling, on hold, needs a closer look, match score 55-69 |
| Critical | `--critical`, `--critical-soft`, `--critical-line`, `--critical-dot` | red | only a missing must-have or a critical error |

Signal colours always sit next to a word (a dot and "Live", a tick and "done"); colour is never the only carrier, and a low match score is plain grey, not red. The earlier token names still work and point at the new values: `--bg` is the ground, `--bg-2` the surface, `--bg-3` the sunken grey, `--needs-you` is the caution amber, `--shadow` is the floating shadow, and `--accent`/`--accent-ink`/`--accent-soft` are the cobalt link colours.

**Type.** Bricolage Grotesque for headings, IBM Plex Sans for everything else, IBM Plex Mono for code. Sizes `--text-xs` 12 to `--text-2xl` 28 (page titles). Line height 1.5 for reading, 1.25 for UI. Counts, scores, dates and tables use tabular numerals (`.tabular`) so columns line up. Above a page title sits an eyebrow: small, uppercase, widely letter-spaced, muted ("Role · Bright Dental", "Editing"); it is written in normal case and raised by CSS, so screen readers read it normally. Table headings are in normal case ("Applicants by source"), 12.5px semibold in `--ink-2` on the `--tint` band (founder decision Q3, 10 October 2026); small capitals stay for eyebrows and section labels. A column of figures is right-aligned, its heading too.

**Space, shape, depth, motion.** A 4-pixel base: `--space-1` 4 to `--space-8` 64. Radius `--r-control` 8 (buttons, fields), `--r-card` 12 (cards, menus), `--r-panel` 16, `--r-pill` for pills. Shadows only on floating layers (the drawer, menus, dialogs, toasts: `--shadow-float`); cards are flat with a hairline border. Motion is CSS only, 150-200 ms with `--ease`, on `transform` and `opacity`; keyframes animate nothing else (the older highlight on a changed draft section aside). Buttons, icon buttons, pill buttons, toggle chips and segmented options shrink to 97% while pressed and spring back in 150 ms. Hover fills apply only on devices with a mouse (`@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`), in the application and on the candidate pages, so nothing stays grey after a tap on a phone. Besides the button spinner and the typing dots, only two loops run: the loading skeleton's fade (opacity, never below 60%) and the ring around a page's own Live dot (transform and opacity). No blur, no `backdrop-filter`, no animation library. With reduced motion asked for, every animation and transition is switched off, pseudo-elements included. `packages/ui/src/weight-guard.test.ts` checks all of this, the stylesheets' byte budgets, and that no package lists a styling, animation, component or icon library. Layers: sticky headers `--z-sticky`, drawer `--z-drawer`, menus `--z-menu`, toasts `--z-toast`.

**Components** (`packages/ui`). `index.tsx` holds the ones without state, safe to use from server components; the ones that listen to the keyboard or keep state live in their own client files and are re-exported from the same place.

- *Button*: primary (near-black), secondary (white, outlined), ghost (no box), danger (soft red, only for actions that cannot be taken back); `quiet` and `destructive` are the earlier names for ghost and danger. Optional leading or trailing icon, a keyboard hint (`kbd="N"`, also announced as the shortcut), a working state (a small spinner and "Working…") and disabled. *IconButton* is an icon alone, always with a spoken label that also shows as a tooltip.
- *Icon*: our own line drawings on a 24-pixel grid, drawn in the text colour: today/home, roles/briefcase, talent/people, interviews/calendar, settings, plus, minus, search, the four chevrons, check, check-circle, x, sparkle/assistant, mail, send, upload, paperclip/attach, mic, eye, edit/pencil, clock, alert, info, help, lock, link, share, filter, sort, more, arrow-left, arrow-right, user, building, star, flag, refresh, external, copy, log-out/sign-out, menu, message, grid. Decorative unless given a label.
- *Avatar*: initials in a circle ("Priya Raman" → PR), sizes sm/md/lg/xl, neutral grey or filled ink (the company and the signed-in person), square for a company. Never a photo for a candidate.
- *Pill*: a status dot and a word, tones neutral, good, caution, critical and accent (accent is ink-outlined, for "in process"); `solid` for a finished state (Hired) and `pulse` only for the one Live pill at the top of a page (a ring grows from the dot and fades; a row that is still applying keeps a still dot and the word). *Chip* is the smaller label for facts and evidence sources. *StatusChip* shows an application's plain-language status as a pill.
- *ScoreBadge*: the match score as "91/100", green from 70, amber 55-69, grey below; screen readers hear "Match score 91 out of 100"; with no score yet it says "No score yet". Bands in our words: Very high 85+, High 70+, Medium 55+, Low below. They describe the number the code worked out and never reuse the AI reading's words (Strong match, Potential match, Weak match, Insufficient evidence), so the two cannot be mistaken for each other on one screen (`helpers.test.ts` checks this). A large version shows the number on its own for the candidate panel. On a list row the coverage ("Based on 5 of 7 requirements") is the badge's tooltip; board cards and the candidate panel print it beside the number.
- *ScoreBars* and *ScoreBar* (`score-bars.tsx`): the score's breakdown, one row per requirement with its tag ("Must-have", or Essential, Important, Helpful), "50% met" and "6 points lost" (or "full marks") in words beside every bar, and an optional detail under it (the finding and the quote, one click away). The bar itself is hidden from screen readers; the words carry the meaning. `barTone`: green when met in full, amber when partly met, red only for a must-have that is not met, grey otherwise (a skill that goes against them is grey, not red). Rows at full marks fold under one line ("4 more at full marks") that opens in place. *ScoreBar* is the single small bar the comparison view uses in a cell. Classes in `tokens.css`: `.sbars`, `.sbars-wrap`, `.sbar`, `.sbar-head`, `.sbar-label`, `.sbar-tag` (`.must`), `.sbar-figures`, `.sbar-lost`, `.sbar-track` (`.sm`, `.critical`), `.sbar-fill` (tones), `.sbar-detail`, `.sbars-more`; and around them `.match-head`, `.match-coverage`, `.match-section` for the candidate panel's **Against your must-haves** card (the big score with its band word and the confidence chip apart, the coverage and what it rests on, the must-have line, red **Missing must-have** chips with the candidate's own words, the bars costliest first, what was left out of the score and never counted against them, the step the board suggests, the version label, and the promise "A match score worked out from checked evidence, with the breakdown shown. In a one-of-many menu (a sort, a job switcher, a stage) the current choice shows a tick before its words as well as being announced as checked. The open menu has a `--line-2` edge so it shows in dark mode. Inside a table's scrolling box (`.table-wrap`) it is placed against the window, so the box never cuts it, follows its button while the page scrolls, and closes once its button is scrolled out of the box. **The rail** (`apps/shell/components/AppShell.tsx`, decisions in `apps/web/src/rail.ts` and `apps/web/src/start-hire.ts`):

- *Company chip*: the company, the person's role in it ("ITS recruiter, limited access" under a grant) and a cog to Settings.
- *Start a hire*: near-black, for the people who may start one (`role.create`). It opens the new-role conversation on Today; nothing is saved until the first message is sent. Pressing **N** anywhere outside a text field does the same, except over an open dialog or menu and on a page where something was typed, ticked or chosen (leaving would throw it away; the button still works there). Because a one-letter key can be set off by speech input or a stray press, Setup's "Keyboard shortcut" card turns it off for that browser (WCAG 2.1.4).
- *Links*: Home, Jobs, Candidates, Interviews, Settings (the same labels in the phone Menu).
- *Jobs in progress* under Jobs, at most eight, then "All jobs" (a live job's link opens its Candidates; a draft or one waiting for approval opens the job): open roles first with a green dot and the number of applicants, then paused, waiting for approval and draft roles with a grey dot, newest first. Each is announced with its state, since the dot and the number are only seen. An interviewer's list comes from the API already limited to the roles they interview for and without counts. An ITS recruiter working inside a customer under a grant gets no list at all: every request they make is recorded for the customer as a page they opened, and the rail would add one on every page load. The list refreshes when a page changes a role and every two minutes while the tab is in view; opening another customer from the recruiter queue empties the cache and redraws the frame, so nothing from the previous company stays on screen.
- *Console* (platform administrators only): Console, with its five sections listed under it like the jobs under Jobs (Overview, Customers, Privacy requests, Problems, System and settings), each with an icon. Console stays marked on all five, and the section open is marked too.
- *Folding*: on a role's own pages (`/roles/<id>` and below) the rail folds to a column of icons. On every other page a small arrow button at the top of the rail folds it the same way (founder answer F3, 15 October 2026); it says whether it is on with `aria-pressed`, and this browser remembers the choice (`ros.rail.collapsed`, read and written inside try/catch, so where nothing can be saved it still folds for the visit). The choice is read after the first draw, so a full page load shows the rail open for a moment before it folds. Folded by choice, `data-collapsed="true"` lets the page widen to 1280px; the console's five links stay in the folded rail as icons with their word. The button is not shown on a job's pages (already folded) or on phones. Under 900px it becomes a top bar: the brand line, Start a hire, a **Menu** button and Sign out stay in the bar, and the links (with the section names and the roles in progress) open below it from Menu. Following a link or pressing Escape closes it.

**The ITS console's layout** (Wave S6, 15 October 2026; `apps/web/src/pages/admin/`, `apps/shell/components/console.css`, loaded only by the console's layout): an Overview at `/admin` and four sections, each its own page and rail link. **Loading.** Skeletons for content that is on its way (a flat block that fades); a table that is reloading keeps its headings and shows quiet rows. A spinner appears only inside a button that is working or beside a step that is under way.

**Accessibility.** WCAG AA contrast (checked by test for both themes), keyboard operability for every action, a visible cobalt focus ring, labels on every input, live regions for the conversation and for toasts, no colour-only meaning, reduced motion respected. Sortable table headings say their order with `aria-sort` on the sorted column only and announce a new order once.

**Mobile.** Owner Mode and the candidate experience are designed at 375px first; Recruiter Mode is desktop-first with a usable phone view for the queue.
